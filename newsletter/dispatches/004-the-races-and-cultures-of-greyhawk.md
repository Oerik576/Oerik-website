# The Races and Cultures of Greyhawk

*Understanding the Peoples Who Shaped the Flanaess*

**by Jon Aerdy**

The kingdoms, cities, and ruins of Greyhawk are the product of thousands of years of migration, conquest, trade, cooperation, and conflict. Ancient empires have disappeared, new kingdoms have risen in their place, and peoples from across Oerth have mingled to create the Flanaess of today.

This week's Dispatch introduces the cultures that shaped that history—from the four great human peoples to the elder races and the wanderers who call the Flanaess home.

## The Four Great Human Cultures

Long before the modern kingdoms arose, the Flanaess was home to the **Flan**, the oldest known human culture of eastern Oerik. Their traditions remain visible in ancient burial mounds, standing stones, sacred places, and the nature faiths that continue throughout the region.

They were eventually joined by three great migrating peoples.

The **Oeridians** became the founders of many of the Flanaess's most powerful kingdoms, including the Great Kingdom of Aerdy. Their legacy survives in castles, noble houses, military traditions, and nations across the continent.

The **Suel** came east following the destruction of their ancient Imperium. Their descendants became especially influential throughout the southern and western Flanaess, while ruins and relics of their lost civilization continue to attract adventurers.

The **Baklunish**, survivors of the same ancient catastrophe that destroyed the Suel Imperium, preserved traditions of scholarship, faith, trade, and personal honor in the western lands.

Together, these four cultures shaped much of the human history of the Flanaess.

## The Elder Races

Humans were never alone upon Oerth.

Elves, dwarves, gnomes, and halflings have inhabited its forests, mountains, hills, and valleys since long before many human kingdoms existed.

The **olvenfolk** guard ancient forests and preserve traditions stretching into the distant past. The **dwur** build their halls beneath mountains and are renowned for craftsmanship, clan loyalty, and determination. The **noniz** establish hidden communities among wooded hills and rocky highlands, while the **hinfolk** build quiet villages among the fertile lands of their larger neighbors.

Each has its own cultures, histories, alliances, and rivalries—and each has left its mark upon the Flanaess.

## Between Two Worlds

The peoples of Greyhawk do not always fit neatly into separate categories.

Half-elves often live between human and elven societies, carrying the heritage of both. Half-orcs face a more difficult existence, particularly along frontiers where old conflicts between humans and orcs remain strong.

For adventuring parties, these mixed heritages reflect something fundamental about Greyhawk: different peoples are constantly meeting, trading, fighting, cooperating, and changing one another.

## The Wanderers

Beyond the great cultures are peoples whose histories follow very different paths.

The **Rhennee** travel the waterways of the Flanaess aboard their distinctive barges. Far to the south, the **Olman** preserve the legacy of ancient jungle civilizations, while the **Touv** inhabit distant lands largely unknown to the peoples of the northern Flanaess.

Countless other tribes, clans, and cultures exist beyond the familiar kingdoms and well-traveled roads.

## A World Shaped by Many Peoples

Greyhawk is not the story of one civilization.

Its kingdoms were built upon layers of older cultures. Its wilderness contains the remnants of peoples long vanished. Its cities bring together travelers whose ancestors may have crossed half of Oerth to reach the lands they now call home.

Understanding those peoples helps explain not only who inhabits the Flanaess, but why its nations, ruins, religions, rivalries, and adventures exist at all.
