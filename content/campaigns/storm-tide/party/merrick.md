Merrick was born in Seaton's Cove, the son of a chandler, and grew up surrounded by ships, sailors, and the working life of the waterfront. From an early age, the sea was simply part of his world.

At the age of ten, Merrick began apprenticing under his older brother Tarmon in the slipways of Keelpoint. He worked alongside experienced shipwrights, learning to select timbers, shape hulls, fit pegs, caulk seams, and recognize the countless small details upon which a vessel's seaworthiness depends.

Years of work in the yards gave Merrick the practical knowledge of a trained journeyman. He learned to recognize rot, structural weakness, poor workmanship, and damage almost instinctively. Even now, he has a habit of running his fingers over wood grain, knocking on beams to listen for hollows, and muttering about "fair lines" and "true keels."

Merrick's life changed when he felt himself called to the service of Osprem, goddess of the sea and sea voyages. He left the shipyard for the shrine, but never abandoned the skills or outlook of his old trade.

Today, Merrick serves Osprem in Seaton's Cove. He blesses ships before they put to sea, tends the seaside shrine, and sometimes mediates disputes among those who make their living along the waterfront.

His knowledge of ships remains an important part of who he is. Merrick can judge the seaworthiness of a vessel, identify construction flaws or sabotage, supervise repairs, and improvise solutions when damaged ships must remain afloat.

Despite choosing the path of faith, Merrick remains quietly proud of his years as a shipwright. Among the craftsmen of Keelpoint, he is remembered as "Salt-Hand"—the boy who left the yard for the shrine, but never entirely left the craft behind.

Merrick is calm, protective, and quietly proud. His connection to both Osprem and the working people of Seaton's Cove makes him a familiar presence along the town's waterfront.
