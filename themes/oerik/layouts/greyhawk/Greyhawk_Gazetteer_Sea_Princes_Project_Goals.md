# Greyhawk Gazetteer: The Hold of the Sea Princes
## Project Goals and Editorial Direction

> **Purpose of this note:** Preserve the project's agreed direction, scope, and working decisions independently of any ChatGPT conversation. This note should guide future writing, editing, and AI-assisted development.

## 1. Vision

Create a comprehensive, fan-created **Dungeon Master's Gazetteer** for **the Hold of the Sea Princes** in the **World of Greyhawk, 576 CY**. The book should present a coherent, believable, and playable maritime nation: its lands and people, rulers and rivalries, commerce and naval power, hidden dangers, and opportunities for adventure.

The Gazetteer is intended as a working regional sourcebook, not simply a setting overview or a collection of adventure hooks. It should help a Dungeon Master understand **how the Hold works** and use that understanding to run campaigns there.

The original **Player's Guide to the Hold of the Sea Princes** is the **first-draft foundation** for this project. Preserve and develop that manuscript rather than starting over.

## 2. Audience and Game Framework

- **Primary audience:** Dungeon Masters.
- **Setting date:** **576 CY**, before the Greyhawk Wars. Later events must not be treated as already having happened.
- **Game framework:** AD&D first-edition sensibilities, with practical game material written for **Adventures Dark and Deep**.
- **Geographic focus:** The Hold itself, including mainland territories, coastal settlements, islands, and its connections to the wider Azure Sea. Southern lands matter especially where they influence the Hold.
- **Tone:** A grounded fantasy maritime setting with political and economic complexity. The **Golden Age of Piracy and the West Indies** are useful historical inspirations, adapted to Greyhawk rather than copied literally. Do not assume commonplace gunpowder weapons.

## 3. Editorial Principles

1. **One unified fan-created setting.** This work draws freely on published Greyhawk material, adventures, fan-created expansions, historical models, and original development. Do **not** label individual setting details as “canon,” “fanon,” or “homebrew” in the narrative.
2. **Internal consistency first.** Geography, chronology, government, trade, law, naval affairs, and social structures should support one another. When existing material conflicts, identify the issue for an editorial decision rather than silently rewriting it.
3. **Protect completed work.** Retain established names, places, institutions, and substantive text unless a change is explicitly approved. Prefer targeted revisions over wholesale rewrites.
4. **Write for DMs.** Include material unavailable to ordinary characters: secrets, conspiracies, faction aims, criminal enterprises, military capabilities, NPC motivations, and adventure possibilities.
5. **Make it usable.** Provide cross-references, maps, tables, game procedures, and location-focused entries where they help at the table.
6. **Avoid needless duplication.** Keep information about a city or region together when practical, rather than scattering its NPCs, conflicts, and locations across several parts.
7. **A bibliography is optional and useful.** General source acknowledgments may appear in the publication, but there is no need for a per-detail provenance register or lore-status labels.

## 4. Four Major Parts

These are **organizational divisions within one Gazetteer**, not separate books. Their boundaries may be adjusted when we prepare the table of contents.

### Part I. The Land and Its People
**Question answered:** What is the Hold, and how did it become what it is?

- History and chronology
- Geography, climate, natural resources, and regions
- Provinces, islands, cities, towns, and smaller settlements
- Population, ancestry, languages, customs, and everyday life
- Religion, temples, and religious institutions
- Agriculture, plantations, industries, and crafts
- Laws, justice, and social institutions
- Roads, rivers, and coastal transportation

### Part II. Power, Politics, and the Maritime World
**Question answered:** Who controls the Hold, and what drives its conflicts?

- Prince Jeon II, the Council of Sea Princes, and regional authorities
- Noble houses, ambitions, alliances, and rivalries
- Government, taxation, and administration
- The Prince's Fleet, Toli Armada, other naval forces, armies, and fortifications
- The Azure Sea trading network, merchant interests, ports, and cargoes
- Independent pirates, pirate fleets, and strongholds
- Privateers, letters of marque, reprisals, and prize adjudication
- Smuggling, criminal networks, and slavery
- Foreign relations, espionage, and diplomacy

### Part III. Campaigning in the Hold
**Question answered:** What adventures and campaigns can take place here?

- Important factions and their objectives
- Notable NPCs, patrons, rivals, and antagonists
- Political conspiracies, maritime conflicts, and hidden agendas
- Pirate organizations and dangerous captains
- Wilderness, ruins, islands, and other adventure locations
- Regional monsters and threats
- Adventure hooks, rumors, and linked campaign frameworks

### Part IV. The Dungeon Master's Toolkit
**Question answered:** What does the DM need to run the setting?

- Regional maps and hex references
- Overland, river, and maritime travel
- Ships, crews, weather, winds, currents, and sea hazards
- Encounter tables by land and sea region
- Ports, settlements, trade goods, cargoes, and services
- Pirate, privateer, naval patrol, and military encounters
- Rumor tables and practical procedures
- Adventures Dark and Deep statistics and rules references

## 5. Maritime World: Established Working Direction

The Hold is **not simply a kingdom of pirates**. Its ruling houses descend from maritime raiders, but by 576 CY it has become an established, prosperous naval and trading power. This transition is central to the Gazetteer's identity.

**Distinct maritime actors:**

- **State and noble fleets:** Defend ports, escort commerce, project power, and enforce territorial claims.
- **Independent pirates:** Raid for themselves, outside lawful commissions; some may operate from remote islands and southern strongholds.
- **Privateers:** Privately outfitted ships acting under limited commissions against specified enemies or under authorized reprisals.
- **Covertly sponsored raiders:** Captains protected or financed by powerful interests whose involvement is publicly denied.

The Hold's wealth now depends heavily on maritime commerce, so piracy can threaten the very economy that grew out of its raiding past. **Piracy, privateering, naval warfare, smuggling, and slave trading are related but distinct activities.** Slavery is a significant social, economic, legal, and political subject, not merely another word for piracy.

**Keoland and the Hold:** Their history includes warfare and deep hostility, but the working portrayal of **576 CY** is **hostile peace**, with trade, rivalry, disputed seizures, and occasional clashes rather than an assumed continuous declared war. The precise authority to issue privateering commissions and the detailed maritime legal system remain subjects for development.

**Prince Jeon II:** His political difficulties, including opposition to slavery and resistance from entrenched interests, are important to preserve and deepen. Do not assume every proposed policy or power has already been finalized.

## 6. The Wider Azure Sea Economy

The Hold participates in a broad trading network rather than dealing only with Keoland.

- **Northern Azure Sea:** Keoland, Principality of Ulek, Pomarj, Onnwal, Idee, and other established coastal markets.
- **Southern Azure Sea and Densac Gulf:** The Hold, Olman Islands, and accessible coastal trading settlements associated with the Scarlet Brotherhood. The Brotherhood's interior is restricted to foreigners in our working interpretation.
- **Farther south:** The Amedio coast and the Sasserine region, relevant to the *Savage Tide* setting. Distant **Zihindia** is an analogue of India; its goods generally reach these markets through intermediaries rather than regular direct voyages from the Hold.

These are useful **commercial groupings**, not new political divisions. The Hold may act as an intermediary and re-export center; not every merchant needs to sail the full distance to a commodity's place of origin.

### Working map and distance assumptions

The regional map uses **30-mile hexes**. The following are **user-provided working route distances**, not independently verified navigational measurements:

| Route | Working distance |
|---|---:|
| Monmurg to Onnwal | 900 miles |
| Port Toli to Sasserine | 360 miles |
| Port Toli to Densac Gulf | 850 miles |
| Onnwal to Idee | 320 miles |
| Densac Gulf to Scarlet Brotherhood | 280 miles |
| Jetsom Island to Sea of Gearnat | 630 miles |

**To clarify later:** Exact endpoints and navigable sailing routes, particularly Port Toli–Sasserine and Port Toli–Densac Gulf. Do not convert these automatically into definitive voyage times without considering route, ship, weather, and stops.

## 7. Existing Draft: How to Use It

The former Player's Guide is **source manuscript material**, not a document to discard. It already includes history, geography, society, rulers and noble houses, settlements, religion, military affairs, trade, and character-creation content.

The prior editorial review identified cleanup tasks: duplicated text, author-facing notes, inconsistent headings and numbering, and verification of character-creation rules. These are **editing tasks**, not reasons to rewrite the book. Some player-oriented character creation may ultimately be moved to an appendix or a separate shorter Player's Guide, but that has **not** been decided.

## 8. Project Workflow in Obsidian

**Obsidian Markdown files stored in Google Drive are the project's persistent working records.** Chat conversations are temporary working sessions, not the authoritative archive.

Recommended practices:

1. Keep this charter at the project root and link to it from a project home/index note.
2. Maintain a **Table of Contents / Outline** note for the evolving publication.
3. Use separate Markdown notes for chapters and, where useful, individual settlements, factions, NPCs, and rules systems.
4. Record important approved decisions in a **Decision Log** with the date, topic, and resulting rule or setting fact.
5. Maintain an **Open Questions** note so proposals are not accidentally treated as decisions.
6. After substantive ChatGPT sessions, save approved text and decisions into the vault promptly. Do not rely on chat history alone.
7. Use Obsidian `[[wikilinks]]` to connect chapters, settlements, people, and institutions.
8. Keep backup/version-history arrangements for the Google Drive vault; synchronization alone is not a substitute for recoverable backups.

## 9. Current Decisions vs. Open Questions

### Decisions already made

- Publication title: **Greyhawk Gazetteer: The Hold of the Sea Princes**.
- A **Dungeon Master's** regional sourcebook, not a player-only guide.
- **576 CY**, before the Greyhawk Wars.
- **Four major parts** as defined above, with flexible boundaries.
- A **single integrated fan-created setting**, without canon/fan labels.
- **Existing manuscript retained** as the project's foundation.
- **Obsidian Markdown in Google Drive** as the persistent working environment.
- Maritime trade, piracy, politics, and slavery are major features of the region.

### Questions not yet settled

- The detailed table of contents and precise chapter order.
- How much of the old character-creation chapter remains in the Gazetteer.
- Exact pirate fleets, their strongholds, and their status in 576 CY.
- Who can lawfully issue letters of marque and how prize courts function.
- Specific trade routes, voyage durations, and commercial regulations.
- The appropriate depth of individual settlement entries and hex mapping.
- The eventual relationship, if any, to a separate shorter Player's Guide.

## 10. Instructions for Future Writing Sessions

When continuing this project, **read this charter, the current outline, the decision log, and the relevant existing chapter before drafting**. Do not recreate completed work from memory. Present structural changes and contradictions for approval. Treat unapproved ideas as proposals, not settled setting facts. Write polished, practical Dungeon Master material in a consistent voice, and update the vault's permanent notes as decisions are finalized.

**Guiding aim:** Build a complete, internally coherent, adventure-ready portrayal of the Hold of the Sea Princes in 576 CY, while protecting and extending the work already accomplished.
