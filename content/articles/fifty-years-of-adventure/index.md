For more than half a century, Dungeons & Dragons has inspired players to gather around a table, create heroes, and explore worlds of imagination. During that time, the game has grown and changed through many editions, each introducing new ideas and new ways to play.

If you are reading OERIK, chances are you already know what Dungeons & Dragons is. You may have started with the original game in the 1970s, discovered Advanced Dungeons & Dragons during the 1980s, learned the hobby through Third or Fourth Edition, or perhaps your first experience came with the enormously popular Fifth Edition.

Each generation found something to love.

## A Hobby Unlike Any Other

What has allowed Dungeons & Dragons to endure for more than fifty years is not simply its rules, settings, or endless stream of adventures. It is the unique experience created whenever a group of friends gathers around a table to tell a story together. No two campaigns are ever exactly alike. Every gaming group develops its own traditions, memorable characters, and legendary tales that become part of its shared history.

Unlike a novel, where every reader follows the same plot, or a video game, where the possibilities are ultimately limited by programming, tabletop role-playing is bounded only by imagination. Players are free to attempt anything they can describe, while the referee brings the world to life and determines the consequences of those choices. Dice introduce uncertainty, but it is the decisions made around the table that create unforgettable adventures.

That freedom has been at the heart of the hobby since the very beginning. Although the rules have evolved over the decades, the essence of Dungeons & Dragons remains unchanged: friends gathering together to explore fantastic worlds, overcome impossible challenges, and create stories that could never have existed without them.

This magazine celebrates that enduring spirit.

This magazine is dedicated to a style of play that traces its roots back to the earliest days of the hobby.

## For the Veterans

If your first adventures took place with First or Second Edition, much of what you find here will feel familiar.

The world is dangerous. Dungeons are mysterious places filled with traps and forgotten treasures. Resource management matters. Light sources burn out, rations run low, and retreat is often the wiser choice than standing and fighting.

Characters are not expected to be larger-than-life heroes from the moment they are created. They earn their reputation through survival, clever thinking, and a little luck.

If that style of adventure sounds familiar, then you are already home.

## For Players of Third and Fourth Edition

Beginning with Third Edition, Dungeons & Dragons expanded dramatically. Skills, feats, prestige classes, and a wide variety of character options allowed players to customize nearly every aspect of their heroes.

Old-school gaming approaches those situations differently.

Rather than searching a character sheet for a specific skill or feat, players are encouraged to describe what they want to do. The referee listens, considers the situation, and decides what happens. Sometimes that means making a simple ruling. Sometimes the dice decide the outcome. Often, a clever idea succeeds without any roll at all.

## For Players of Fifth Edition

Modern Fifth Edition emphasizes heroic fantasy. Characters quickly become powerful, capable individuals who are expected to overcome tremendous challenges and shape the fate of the world.

Classic Dungeons & Dragons presents a different experience.

Beginning adventurers are not superheroes. A goblin with a spear is a genuine threat. A single wrong decision can have lasting consequences. Combat is often swift and dangerous, and many experienced players will tell you that the smartest victory is the one won without drawing a sword.

Success comes not from overwhelming powers, but through planning, caution, teamwork, and ingenuity.

## A Different Philosophy

The style of play celebrated by OERIK assumes that the world exists independently of the player characters.

The dungeon was not built for them.

The monsters do not scale to their level.

The ancient dragon does not care that the party has only just begun its journey.

Sometimes the wisest decision is to negotiate. Sometimes it is to flee. Sometimes it is simply to come back later with more experience and better equipment.

This approach creates a world that feels alive, unpredictable, and full of genuine discovery.

## More Than Rules

The purpose of this magazine is not to argue that one edition of Dungeons & Dragons is better than another. Every generation of the game has brought new players into the hobby and created unforgettable memories around the gaming table.

Instead, OERIK celebrates one particular branch of that long tradition: the classic style of fantasy role-playing that inspired the World of Greyhawk and continues to thrive today.

Whether you are returning after decades away or discovering old-school gaming for the very first time, we invite you to step beyond the city walls, light your torch, and see what waits in the darkness beyond.

*The adventure begins there.*
