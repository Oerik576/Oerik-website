**Wreckshore, Salinmoor --- 16 Flocktime, 576 CY**\
**Session 7 --- March 21, 2026**

**Adventurers:** Cyr Baggins, Darrak Goldenforge, Galborn Valgwyn, Haldir Sorenson, Hugo Baudelaire, Brother Merrick, Xahlen Thistlewacker\
**Level:** 2\
**Adventure:** RX02B --- *The Camp on Wreckshore Beach Part 3 - Healing the Ley Line Knot*\
**Weather:** Mild and clear; cool morning air giving way to scattered clouds, a steady breeze off the Azure Sea, and an afternoon high near 72°F

## The Arrival of Ethyra

Two weeks passed after Worliwynn’s temporary suppression of the Knot.

The whispers did not return.

Then word came from **Worliwynn** asking the adventurers to meet her at **Stone Ring Pond**. When they arrived, the gnome druid was already waiting within the ancient ring, the still water reflecting the stones and open sky.

“The call was sent,” she told them. “Now we wait. If Ehlonna favors us, help will come.”

They did not have to wait long.

Near the edge of the pond stood an enormous bronzewood tree, its trunk broad enough that several men could not have encircled it with joined hands. The bark shifted.

Not split. Shifted.

For a moment the surface of the tree seemed to ripple like water. Then a figure stepped forward through the living wood as though passing through a curtain.

The woman who emerged was tall and imposing, dressed in robes of sea-blue and earth-brown. Living ivy curled across the fabric at her shoulders. Her skin had the warm bronze cast of someone who had spent a lifetime beneath the open sky, and her black hair was streaked with silver and braided with oak leaves, shells, and pieces of driftwood. At her side rested a scythe of dark metal wrapped with mistletoe and greenery.

Worliwynn bowed her head.

“**Ethyra, Voice of Oak and Sea. Archdruid of the Circle of the Oaken Seal.**”

Ethyra’s gaze moved across the adventurers before settling on Worliwynn.

“You held the Knot?”

“For now.”

Ethyra nodded once, then looked back to the party.

“You held corruption at bay when it might have spread through the land. The Oerth owes you gratitude.”

There was no ceremony in the words. She spoke them as a simple fact.

Haldir glanced toward Worliwynn. “Can you heal it?”

“Not alone.”

Ethyra stepped farther from the tree. “A wound such as this lies between worlds. It is life and spirit, but it is also power shaped by magic. Druidic strength can guide the living current, but arcane discipline is needed to bind and stabilize what has been damaged.”

Hugo understood immediately where the conversation was going.

“You need a wizard.”

“A powerful one.”

Worliwynn smiled faintly. “We may have arranged that.”

Ethyra looked toward the west.

“A member of the Circle of Eight is coming.”

That silenced the group.

The journey from Stone Ring Pond back to Wreckshore took roughly two hours. Ethyra spoke little along the way, preferring to observe the land as they traveled. When they reached the fishing camp, she did not go directly beneath the cabin. Instead, she removed a small carved totem from among her belongings, knelt near the edge of the camp, and pressed it into the earth.

Her eyes closed.

For several minutes she remained perfectly still.

Then she exhaled.

“I do not sense the return of corruption.”

Worliwynn visibly relaxed.

“The suppression still holds,” Ethyra continued. “That gives us time.”

That evening, a large fire was built upon the beach. Food was prepared, bedrolls spread nearby, and for the first time since Ethyra’s arrival the gathering felt less like a council and more like travelers sharing a camp. The sea was calm beyond the firelight, and the conversation eventually turned to what would happen when the permanent rite began.

Haldir asked the question first.

“Why does it require both a druid and a wizard?”

Ethyra looked toward the dark water.

“Because the Knot is not one thing. It is current and spirit. Magic and life. To mend only one half would leave the other broken.”

Darrak leaned forward. “And what happens to us while you’re doing this?”

“The corruption will resist.”

Ethyra’s answer was immediate.

“You will stand guard. Whatever force has taken root there will not simply permit us to cut it away.”

Merrick studied her across the fire. “And if the ritual fails?”

For the first time, Ethyra’s expression hardened.

“Then the corruption will spread again. The land will sour around it. In time, the wound may become so deeply rooted that no one living could heal it.”

Silence settled around the fire.

Eventually Hugo asked about the ritual itself.

Ethyra reached into a leather pouch and withdrew a dark piece of wood, polished smooth with age.

“The **Heartwood of the First Tree**,” she said. “My offering.”

She returned it carefully to the pouch.

“The wizard will bring two others: the **Tear of the Sea Mother** and the **Moon’s Breath Crystal**.”

Haldir frowned. “Root. Tide. Sky.”

“Yes.”

“The same pattern as Worliwynn’s rite.”

“A greater expression of it.”

Ethyra looked from Haldir to the others.

“Root. Tide. Sky. And sacrifice. All must be woven together.”

No one asked what she meant by the last word.

Not yet.

The fire burned low as the night deepened, and eventually everyone settled around it to sleep. Somewhere beyond the circle of light, waves moved steadily against the shore.

The temporary suppression still held.

Tomorrow, if Vandy’s message had reached its destination, the other half of the ritual would arrive.

## The Arrival of Nystul

Morning came clear over Wreckshore, the fire on the beach reduced to a bed of coals and ash. The party had barely begun to gather itself when figures appeared approaching the camp.

**Vandy Banderdash** came first, accompanied by a broad-shouldered knight in travel-worn armor. Behind them walked a man whose bearing made it immediately clear that no ordinary scholar had answered Vandy’s message.

The knight stepped forward before anyone else could speak.

“Sir Darrien Kestrel,” he announced, giving the party a formal nod. Then he turned slightly toward the wizard beside him. “Sworn sword and retainer to **Nystul**, member of the Circle of Eight, Master of Light and Shadow, Keeper of Arcane Mysteries, and Defender of Balance.”

Nystul endured the introduction with the expression of a man who had heard it far too many times.

Then he looked toward the cabin.

“You have my attention,” he said. “Let us see this corruption you’ve stirred me from my work to mend.”

Ethyra approached him before the party could answer. The two exchanged a few quiet words beyond easy hearing, each studying the other with the careful familiarity of powerful people accustomed to working beside equals. Worliwynn joined them briefly, describing what she had sensed beneath Wreckshore and confirming that the temporary suppression still held.

Sir Darrien remained with the adventurers.

“My master will want the magical details,” he said. “I want the part that tried to kill you.”

That proved considerably easier to explain.

The party described the first whispers beneath the cabin, the discovery of the Chikleek lair, and the battle that had followed. They told him about the corrupted roots and the strange pressure beneath the cavern, and how the whispers had eventually returned even after the creature itself had been destroyed.

Darrien listened closely, interrupting only when he wanted a detail clarified.

“So the voices returned before Worliwynn examined the site?”

Cyr nodded. “They stopped whenever I left Wreckshore. Started again when I came back.”

“And the creature beneath the cabin?”

“Dead,” Darrak said.

Darrien glanced toward the others. “You are certain?”

Darrak looked at him.

“Yes.”

That appeared to satisfy him.

While the conversation continued, **Hugo** became aware of another sound.

*Click… whirr… grind.*

He looked toward Nystul.

Nothing.

*Click… whirr… grind.*

The noise was faint and irregular, almost lost beneath the sound of the surf and the conversation around him. Hugo tilted his head, listening more carefully.

“Do you hear that?”

Cyr looked at him. “Hear what?”

Hugo raised one hand for silence.

There it was again.

*Click… whirr… grind.*

His eyes tracked the sound through empty air.

Nystul noticed.

The wizard stopped speaking with Ethyra and looked toward Hugo. “Something wrong?”

“There’s something moving near you.”

For the first time since arriving, Nystul seemed genuinely surprised.

Hugo pointed toward a patch of apparently empty air. “There. Something mechanical. Small gears, I think. One of them is catching.”

Nystul stared at him for a moment.

“You can hear that?”

Hugo frowned. “Shouldn’t I?”

“No.”

Nystul lifted one hand and made a small motion with his fingers.

The empty air shimmered.

A small brass-and-iron sphere appeared hovering beside him, its surface covered in fine runes and tiny mechanical fittings. Delicate wings extended from its sides, while a single arcane lens turned toward Hugo. Small tools and articulated arms folded neatly beneath its body.

“This is **Xeril**,” Nystul said.

Hugo stared at the construct with immediate fascination.

Xeril stared back.

*Click… whirr… grind.*

Hugo smiled faintly. “That.”

Nystul’s expression changed from surprise to interest.

“Most people cannot hear Xeril at all when he is concealed.”

“Apparently he isn’t as quiet as he thinks he is.”

Sir Darrien covered what might have been the beginning of a smile.

Nystul studied Hugo more carefully now, clearly reassessing him. Hugo was still a young wizard by any reasonable measure, but noticing a hidden mechanical construct by the irregularity of its internal workings was not something Nystul had expected.

For a moment it looked as though he might begin asking questions.

Then Ethyra stepped beside him.

“The Knot.”

Nystul glanced toward her, then back to Hugo.

“Yes.”

He allowed Xeril to fade from sight again.

“This can wait,” he said. “The ley-line corruption cannot.”

He turned toward the cabin.

“Let’s go see what we are dealing with.”

The others gathered their equipment and followed. Whatever curiosity had passed between Hugo and Nystul would have to remain unanswered for now.

Beneath the cabin, the wounded Knot was waiting.

## The Knot Chamber

The passage beneath the cabin descended into the old Chikleek lair and then opened into the cavern where the corruption had first taken hold. The chamber looked different now. The sickly green pulse that had once moved through the roots was gone, and the great mass along the cavern wall had shriveled into something dry and brittle. Withered roots clung to the stone, tangled among patches of dead fungus, but the deeper vibration remained—a slow pressure beneath the floor that could still be felt through boots and fingertips.

**Ethyra** entered first and stood quietly near the center of the chamber. **Nystul** followed, his attention moving from the dead root mass to the surrounding stone and then back again. **Worliwynn** remained close, watching both of them as they examined the place she had only been able to suppress.

For several minutes there was little conversation.

Ethyra crouched beside the roots and pressed one hand against the floor. Nystul paced slowly through the chamber, stopping at intervals to study the stone and the faint traces of power still moving through it. Somewhere near him, unseen once more, **Xeril** gave the occasional soft *click… whirr… grind* that only Hugo seemed able to notice.

At last Ethyra stood and pointed to an open stretch of ground near the mass of roots.

“Here.”

Nystul looked toward the place she had chosen.

“The current passes beneath it?”

“It gathers close enough.”

He studied the space for another moment, then nodded.

The others began clearing room for the rite, moving loose stone and old debris away from the area while Ethyra and Nystul discussed the placement of the circle. The cavern remained unnaturally still around them. Whatever Worliwynn’s suppression had done, it was still holding.

Then **Haldir** heard movement behind them.

Not from the roots.

From the passage.

He turned.

A white heron was walking into the cavern.

Galborn saw it a moment later and immediately recognized the bird. It was the same noble heron he had led Haldir to in the wetlands, the same creature Haldir had spoken with beneath the magic of *Speak with Animals*. It had freely given the feather they used as the Token of Sky.

Neither man had brought it here.

The heron continued forward without hesitation, stepping through the cavern as though it knew exactly where it was going. No one moved to stop it. Even Nystul fell silent as the bird crossed the chamber.

It passed Haldir and Galborn, then walked directly toward the place Ethyra had chosen for the ritual.

There it stopped.

The bird stood beside the forming circle, white feathers bright against the dead roots and dark stone, and calmly watched the gathered druids, wizard, and adventurers around it.

Haldir looked toward Worliwynn.

She did not seem surprised.

Whatever role the heron had played in helping suppress the Knot, it had not finished with Wreckshore yet.

## The Ritual

With the chamber cleared, **Ethyra** took her place near the center of the chosen ground. The white heron remained close, standing with unnatural calm beside the ritual space while **Nystul** moved opposite the archdruid. **Worliwynn**, **Vandy**, and the adventurers formed a loose perimeter around them, leaving enough room for the rite to unfold.

Ethyra began first.

Her voice was low and measured, the words spoken in an old druidic tongue that seemed to settle into the stone rather than echo from it. She removed a dark piece of ancient wood from her pouch and placed it carefully at her feet.

“The **Heartwood of the First Tree**,” she said. “It attaches us to the Knot.”

Nystul gave a small signal.

Something moved in the empty air beside him.

Hugo heard the familiar *click… whirr… grind* before Xeril became visible. The brass-and-iron construct unfolded several delicate arms and projected a faint ring of runes across the cavern floor. Nystul followed the pattern slowly, walking the circumference and tracing lines of pale light through the air. One by one they settled onto the stone until a glowing seven-pointed star—the **Septagram**—surrounded the ritualists.

Ethyra continued her chant while Nystul completed the final line.

Then she produced the second offering.

It was a clear sapphire shaped like a teardrop. When she placed it upon the western side of the Septagram, the sound of distant surf passed through the cavern. **Merrick** recognized the symbolism immediately.

“The **Tear of the Sea Mother**,” he said.

The gem gleamed with a faint inner light. Osprem’s sorrow, preserved in crystal, became the offering of Tide.

The roots along the cavern wall moved.

At first the motion was so slight that only Haldir, Galborn, and Worliwynn seemed to notice it. A dead vine tightened against the stone. Another slowly curled upon itself.

Worliwynn’s expression changed.

“That should not be happening.”

Darrien looked toward her.

“The vines are dead.”

A deeper vibration passed through the floor.

Nystul did not stop. He reached into his robes and withdrew the final token: a pale crystal that seemed almost colorless until it crossed the light of the Septagram. Then silver radiance appeared within it like moonlight trapped under ice.

“The **Moon’s Breath Crystal**,” he said.

He placed it at the northern point of the star.

Light spread immediately through the entire pattern.

For a moment the chamber was perfectly still.

Then the Septagram flared.

Its seven points burned like stars in the gloom as every withered root along the cavern wall snapped taut at once. Dry wood groaned against stone. The roots twisted upward, writhing over one another and knotting together into a great circular arch. Bark split beneath the strain and, impossibly, fresh green sap began to seep from cracks in wood that had appeared dead only moments before.

Sir Darrien drew his sword.

“Whatever you’re going to do,” he called to Nystul, “do it fast.”

Within the arch, the air shimmered.

Blue, violet, and silver spread across empty space like oil upon water. The colors folded through one another, growing brighter until the surface became a shifting veil suspended inside the living frame of roots. Light from the Septagram struck it and bent strangely, twisting reflections into shapes that the eye could not quite follow.

The white heron lifted its head.

It gave a single sharp cry.

The chamber turned cold.

A faint breeze moved across the cavern, carrying the smells of autumn leaves, damp earth, and something sharp and metallic—iron at the edge of taste. The surface within the arch steadied, becoming less like light and more like a window.

Beyond it, shadows moved.

The adventurers tightened their line in front of the Septagram. Whatever the corruption had opened onto, something on the other side had noticed the ritual.

And it was coming through.

## The First Assault

The portal steadied within the arch of twisted roots, its surface shifting in blue, violet, and silver as though another world had been pressed against the cavern and held there by force. For one heartbeat nothing came through. Then a shudder passed across the opening and the cold deepened. Guttural cries erupted from beyond the veil, followed by the scrape of feet and the glitter of crystalline blades. The first of the creatures burst into the chamber—small, goblin-like shapes with pale, malicious faces and weapons raised—followed quickly by more until ten of them had spilled across the stone. Behind them came two great hounds made half of shadow, their eyes burning with sickly green fire. They lowered their heads, took in the defenders, and began to spread apart as though already searching for the weakest place in the line.

One stumbled and fell as roots bound its knees together; another slashed frantically at the vegetation while a third was dragged backward as the growth tightened around its legs. Yet the spell did not take them all. Several broke clear before the roots could close around them, and those that remained free continued toward the defensive line with shrill cries and raised blades.

Worliwynn saw them coming. She swept one hand across the battlefield and spoke a short druidic invocation. Pale radiance blossomed over the advancing fey. **Faerie Fire** clung to their bodies in shimmering outlines, tracing arms, weapons, and hunched shoulders in unnatural light. Even those struggling within Haldir’s entangling roots now stood out clearly against the darker stone, while the unbound attackers found concealment suddenly impossible. “There,” Worliwynn called to the others. “Now you can see them properly.” The glowing outlines transformed the confused rush from the portal into distinct targets and gave the defenders a precious advantage as the first creatures reached them.

**Brother Merrick** raised his holy symbol above the line and invoked a blessing over the defenders. His voice carried through the cavern above the shrieks of the trapped fey and the grinding sound of Xeril maintaining the Septagram. The prayer was brief, but its effect settled over the party like a steadying hand. Fear and uncertainty gave way to focus as swords were raised, bows drawn, and shields set. Behind them, Ethyra and Nystul remained within the glowing star, continuing the ritual as though the fighting were happening at a great distance rather than only yards away.

Then the Dark Hounds threw back their heads.

Their baying did not sound like the cry of any natural beast. It rolled through the cavern like something pulled from a nightmare, deep enough to be felt in the chest and sharp enough to make instinct demand flight. For an instant the defensive line wavered as the sound struck them. **Vandy Banderdash** reacted immediately. She raised one hand and invoked **Delleb**, calling not for strength of arm but for clarity of thought. “Hold to what you know,” she called. “Fear is not truth.” Her prayer broke the grip of the unnatural terror where it had begun to take hold, restoring reason before panic could tear gaps in the formation.

By the second exchange, the battle had divided itself into two struggles. Near the portal, many of the Winter Court goblinoids remained trapped in Haldir’s spell, hacking uselessly at the roots or straining against them while Faerie Fire made every movement painfully visible. The creatures that had escaped the entanglement rushed the defenders instead, joined by the Dark Hounds, which bounded forward with far greater speed than their smaller companions. The adventurers met them between the portal and the Septagram, exactly where Ethyra had warned they must. Steel struck crystal-edged weapons, arrows and spells crossed the chamber, and the first line of the Winter Court found itself checked before it could reach the ritualists.

Behind that clash, the Septagram continued to burn steadily. Ethyra’s chant never stopped, and Nystul’s voice had joined hers in a lower counterpoint while Xeril hovered above the runes, maintaining their light despite the faint irregular grind Hugo could still hear within its mechanisms. The first assault had been contained, but no one mistook that for victory. The portal remained open, the shadows beyond it continued to move, and the defenders had already learned what their task truly was: not to defeat everything that came through, but to make certain nothing reached the circle.

## The Sacrifice

The first assault had barely been checked when **Ethyra’s** voice rose above the clash of steel and the shrieks of the Winter Court creatures. She and **Nystul** remained within the Septagram while the defenders fought only yards away, its blue-white lines still burning beneath their feet. Without interrupting the cadence of the rite, each drew a narrow obsidian knife. Nystul opened his palm first, allowing his blood to fall across the nearest runes; Ethyra followed, and where the drops struck the glowing lines the light deepened and spread. The Septagram seemed to draw the blood into itself, the seven-pointed star pulsing once beneath the cavern floor as though something below had answered.

Then Ethyra turned toward the white heron.

For **Haldir** and **Galborn**, the moment carried a meaning the others could not fully share. They knew this bird. Weeks earlier Galborn had led them to its nesting grounds, and Haldir had spoken with it beneath the magic of *Speak with Animals*. The heron had understood that the land was sick and had freely offered one of its feathers so that Worliwynn could suppress the wound beneath Wreckshore. Now the same bird had come of its own accord into the cavern and taken its place within the ritual circle. There had been no cage, no rope, and no hand forcing it forward.

Ethyra knelt beside it and rested one hand gently against its breast. Her prayer was quiet enough that only those nearest the circle could hear the ancient druidic words. The heron did not struggle. It simply stood before her, still and watchful, while the fighting continued beyond the circle.

When Ethyra drew the blade across its throat, the act was swift.

Several of the defenders faltered for an instant at the sight of it. The sacrifice was nothing like the symbolic offerings of acorn, shell, and crystal that had come before. Blood flowed across Ethyra’s hands and onto the glowing lines of the Septagram, joining the blood already given by the two ritualists. The smell of salt, earth, and iron filled the chamber as Ethyra lowered the bird carefully rather than casting it aside.

The reaction from the circle was immediate. White light surged through the runes so brightly that the combatants nearest it turned their faces away. For a heartbeat the entire star seemed incandescent; then the brilliance settled into a deeper, steadier glow, stronger than before. The blood did not remain pooled upon the stone. It vanished slowly into the lines as though the Oerth itself were drawing the offering downward.

No explanation could make the sight easy to accept, but the effect made its purpose impossible to dismiss. The rite demanded more than symbols. Something living had been given back to the land, and the Knot had answered.

Worliwynn watched the Septagram brighten and spoke without taking her eyes from it. “Life feeds life. Death feeds life as well. The balance does not end simply because we dislike one half of it.”

There was little time to consider her words. The portal reacted violently to the strengthening circle. Its surface convulsed, colors tearing across one another as the root arch groaned against the cavern wall. Whatever waited beyond had felt the change.

And it answered.
