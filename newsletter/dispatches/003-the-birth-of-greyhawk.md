# The Birth of Greyhawk

*How Gary Gygax's Home Campaign Became Dungeons & Dragons First Fantasy Setting*

**by R. K. Zunt**

Greyhawk occupies a unique place in the history of role-playing games. It was not simply a fantasy world created for Dungeons & Dragons. It grew alongside the game itself, beginning as Gary Gygax's home campaign and gradually becoming one of the foundations of the hobby.

This week's Dispatch follows that journey—from the earliest experiments that led to Dungeons & Dragons through Castle Greyhawk, Supplement I, and the publication of the World of Greyhawk.

## The World Before Greyhawk

In the early 1970s, fantasy role-playing games did not yet exist. Gary Gygax and Jeff Perren were developing *Chainmail*, while Dave Arneson was experimenting with adventures in which players controlled individual heroes rather than armies.

Those ideas eventually came together in the original *Dungeons & Dragons* in 1974. The new game provided rules for fantastic adventures but almost no official campaign setting.

Gary Gygax already had one.

## Castle Greyhawk

At the center of Gygax's campaign stood the Free City of Greyhawk and the sprawling dungeon beneath Castle Greyhawk.

The dungeon wasn't designed around a predetermined story. It was a changing environment filled with factions, traps, secret passages, monsters, and treasure. Players explored it, survived it, and changed it through their actions.

Characters and legends that would eventually become part of Greyhawk lore first emerged through actual play around Gygax's table.

## The First Greyhawk

As the campaign developed, new rules were created to handle what happened during play.

In 1975, many of those ideas appeared in *Dungeons & Dragons Supplement I: Greyhawk*. Despite the name, it was not yet a campaign setting. It introduced and expanded game concepts such as the Thief and Paladin classes, variable weapon damage, spells, monsters, magic items, and combat options.

For many early players, "Greyhawk" was first encountered not as a map, but on the cover of a rulebook.

## From Dungeon to World

Greyhawk gradually expanded beyond the city and castle.

The world became Oerth. Its great continent was Oerik, and the eastern portion where most Greyhawk adventures took place became known as the Flanaess.

Instead of defining every corner of the setting, Gygax left enormous spaces for referees to develop themselves. Kingdoms, wilderness, ancient ruins, rival powers, and unexplored territories provided the framework. The individual campaign supplied the details.

## The World of Greyhawk

In 1980, TSR published the *World of Greyhawk Fantasy World Setting*, now commonly called the Folio. For the first time, players could see the kingdoms and wilderness of the Flanaess together on Darlene Pekul's famous maps.

The setting expanded again with the 1983 *World of Greyhawk Fantasy Game Setting*. Nations, history, calendars, deities, politics, weather, encounters, and other details transformed Greyhawk into a richer campaign world without eliminating the open spaces that referees could make their own.

That version of Greyhawk stands at a particularly important moment: **576 Common Year**.

## Why Greyhawk Endures

Greyhawk was not created as a scripted story. It grew through play—one dungeon, expedition, kingdom, and legend at a time.

Later publications would advance the timeline, reshape nations, introduce new conflicts, and carry Greyhawk into new eras. But OERIK begins with the classic World of Greyhawk in **CY 576**, when the great wars have not yet reshaped the Flanaess and its future remains unwritten.

It is a world waiting for the adventurers at the table to decide what happens next.
