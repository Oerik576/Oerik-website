Xalen Thistlewacker was raised among the rocky hills west of the Lortmil Mountains, near the city of Tringlee. His family has long made its living as merchants, carrying goods along the roads and mountain passes connecting the Ulek States with Keoland, the Gran March, and the communities of the Kron Hills.

The Thistlewackers were not wealthy merchant princes, but successful traveling traders who understood that knowing what people needed—and where to find it—could be more valuable than owning a warehouse full of goods. Xalen learned to judge merchandise, keep accounts, bargain over prices, and recognize when someone was trying to cheat him.

Traveling with merchant caravans also taught him less respectable but equally useful skills. He learned how to move unnoticed through a crowded marketplace, keep valuables hidden, open a stubborn lock, and recognize the tricks employed by thieves and dishonest traders.

Life on the road was not always peaceful. Bandits, goblins, and other dangers were an accepted risk of carrying valuable goods between distant settlements. Xalen learned to defend himself and the family wagons, eventually becoming as comfortable with weapons as he was with a merchant's ledger.

Like many gnomes, Xalen also possessed an aptitude for illusion magic. What began as simple fascination developed into serious study, giving him another useful tool for protecting himself, confusing enemies, and gaining an advantage when cleverness was preferable to a drawn blade.

Xalen's strongest connection beyond his immediate family is through his mother's kin in the Kron Hills. His uncle, Alvyn Bannockburn, has long been an important figure in his life and a connection to the larger Rock Gnome communities east of the Lortmils. Visits to his mother's family gave Xalen relatives and contacts scattered across the central Sheldomar Valley.

As Xalen grew older, the familiar merchant routes began to seem smaller. Years spent hearing caravan guards, travelers, and adventurers exchange stories around roadside fires awakened his curiosity about the places beyond his family's normal trade routes.

Eventually, Xalen left the family business to travel on his own. He brought with him the practical habits of a merchant, the caution of someone raised on dangerous roads, a talent for illusion, and the decidedly gnomish belief that a clever solution is usually preferable to an obvious one.

His travels eventually carried him south toward Seaton's Cove, where opportunity, trade, and rumors of unusual happenings offered considerably more excitement than another journey between familiar markets.

For Xalen Thistlewacker, adventuring is partly a search for fortune—but perhaps more importantly, it is a chance to finally discover for himself whether all those stories he heard growing up were actually true.
