# The Old School Renaissance

*Keeping the Torch Burning*

**by Roger E. Moerik**

The Old School Renaissance—usually shortened to OSR—is not a single game, company, or organization. It is a community built around the traditions of early fantasy role-playing and a shared approach to adventure that continues to evolve more than fifty years after the birth of the hobby.

This week's Dispatch looks at what the OSR actually is, the principles that connect its many different games, and how both classic and modern rules systems continue that tradition today.

## What Is the OSR?

The OSR celebrates a style of gaming built around exploration, player ingenuity, meaningful risk, and open-ended adventure. Some players still use rules published in the 1970s and 1980s, while others play entirely new systems inspired by those games.

What connects them is less a particular rulebook than a shared philosophy of play. The OSR can look backward to the earliest days of the hobby while still producing new adventures, campaign settings, rules systems, and ideas.

## Common Themes of OSR Play

Exploration comes first. Players are encouraged to observe, experiment, negotiate, and solve problems rather than rely solely on abilities written on a character sheet.

Player ingenuity matters. Referees make rulings when the rules cannot anticipate an unexpected idea, and danger carries genuine consequences.

Most importantly, the campaign world exists independently of the characters. Not every monster is meant to be fought, not every location is appropriate for the party's level, and knowing when to retreat can be just as important as knowing when to draw a sword.

## The Original Games

Old-school gaming never disappeared. Groups continue to play Original Dungeons & Dragons, the Basic and Expert rules, and both First and Second Edition Advanced Dungeons & Dragons.

Each version approaches fantasy role-playing somewhat differently. Some emphasize simplicity and flexibility, while others offer greater depth and variety. Together they established many of the traditions that continue to shape OSR campaigns today.

## Modern OSR Games

The OSR has also produced an entire generation of new games.

OSRIC helped open the door for modern old-school publishing, followed by systems such as Basic Fantasy RPG, Labyrinth Lord, Swords & Wizardry, White Box, Old-School Essentials, Adventures Dark & Deep™, and Shadowdark RPG.

Some recreate classic rules. Some reorganize and clarify them. Others combine old-school principles with newer ideas.

Together they demonstrate that the OSR is not simply about preserving the past. It remains an active and evolving part of the role-playing hobby.

## The Adventure Continues

The OSR is ultimately less about nostalgia than about keeping a particular style of adventure alive.

Rules may differ from table to table, but exploration, meaningful choices, referee judgment, player ingenuity, and genuine risk remain at its heart.

More than fifty years after fantasy role-playing began, there are still unexplored maps, unopened doors, and adventures waiting on the other side.
