**Session 7 --- March 21, 2026**

**Adventurers:** Cyr Baggins, Darrak Goldenforge, Galborn Valgwyn, Haldir Sorenson, Hugo Baudelaire, Brother Merrick, Xahlen Thistlewacker\
**Level:** 2\
**Adventure:** RX02B --- *The Camp on Wreckshore Beach Part 3 - Healing the Ley Line Knot*\
**Weather:** Mild and clear; cool morning air giving way to scattered clouds, a steady breeze off the Azure Sea, and an afternoon high near 72°F

## The Second Assault

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/storm-tide/journals/007-journal/G-03-Boggles-REdcaps-Ogres.webp"
    alt="The defenders battle Boggles, Redcaps, and a Winter Court Ogre while the healing ritual continues."
    caption="The second assault drives Boggles, Redcaps, and a Winter Court Ogre against the defenders."
    type="wide"

The second wave hit before the defenders had fully reset their line. The chamber was already choked with struggling goblinoids from **Haldir’s Entangle**, their legs wrapped in root and vine where the spell had taken hold beyond the portal’s immediate fey-tainted radius. Some still fought to tear themselves free, but the **Redcaps** had no interest in helping them. Laughing in shrill, hateful voices, they fell upon the trapped creatures with their sickles, chopping down anything tangled at their feet simply for the joy of drawing blood. Around them the **Boggles** spread out low and fast, their long limbs skittering over the cavern floor as slime trailed behind them, while the **Winter Court Ogre** forced its way farther into the chamber, pale-blue skin steaming in the cold air and its frozen club dragging a line of frost across the stone.

The party answered at once. **Galborn** had already marked the largest threat; **Emberthorn** flared faintly in his hands as he drew and loosed in one practiced motion, the arrow streaking past the melee to strike the ogre high in the shoulder. The brute roared, more angry than truly hurt, but the shot turned its head and slowed its advance long enough for **Darrak** to plant himself squarely in its path. Shield forward and weapon raised, the dwarf met the oncoming giant with the grim resolve of a man who had decided there would be no retreat through him. Beside him, **Merrick** stepped into place with Osprem’s blessing still fresh upon his lips, his mace lifted and his voice carrying over the clash. “Hold fast! By wave and storm, hold fast!” The blessing seemed to settle over the defenders like a taut cord, stiffening arms and steadying nerves.

At the same time, **Worliwynn’s Faerie Fire** still clung to several of the enemy like ghostly foxfire, a cold outline that made their movements easier to follow in the dimness. One Boggle, limned in that eldritch glow, tried to slither around the flank only to find **Cyr** already moving to intercept it. The halfling vanished briefly among taller bodies and reappeared at the creature’s side, driving his blade home under its ribs before ducking away from its flailing claws. Not far from him, **Xahlen** slipped in low and quick, using the chaos created by the Entangle and the glowing outlines to pick his moment. Rather than overcommit against the larger monsters, he struck one of the goblinoids that had broken free of the vines, hamstringing it and sending it sprawling into the roots again. **Haldir**, who had cast the spell that had fouled the first rush, did not waste the advantage. He met another goblinoid near the edge of the snaring growth and brought it down with a hard, efficient blow, never quite taking his attention off the ritual circle beyond.

In the rear, **Hugo** raised his hand and snapped off a quick incantation. A pulse of force leapt from his fingers and hammered into a charging Boggle, knocking it backward just as it gathered itself to spring toward the circle. Near him, **Vandy Banderdash** tightened her grip on her holy symbol of Delleb and called out a single sharp word of command. One of the Redcaps lurched as if struck across the face by the sound itself, checking its advance for a precious instant. **Sir Darrien** used that hesitation immediately, stepping in with shield and sword to keep it from reaching the ritualists. Behind him, **Nystul** and **Ethyra** never broke cadence. Their voices twined together over the Septagram while the blood-fed runes burned steadily beneath their feet, and if either felt the fight tightening around them, neither showed it.

Then the ogre answered.

With a snarl like breaking ice, the Winter Court brute heaved its frozen club overhead and brought it smashing down against the cavern floor. The impact cracked through the chamber like a felled tree. White frost exploded outward in a fan before it, racing over stone and root alike. The ground glazed in an instant, and the air turned bitterly cold. **Darrak** braced and took the shock through his boots, but even he slid half a step as rime climbed his greaves. **Merrick** staggered to one knee before catching himself with his shield, while **Xahlen** and **Galborn**, caught at the edge of the spreading freeze, felt their legs drag as if the cold itself had taken hold of their joints. For a moment the front of the battle slowed under that wave of winter magic, and the ogre bellowed in triumph, pushing forward under the shroud of its numbing aura.

The Unseelie line surged behind it. A pair of Boggles took advantage of the frozen ground to skitter around the melee and spit slime toward the defenders near the circle. One spray splashed wide across the stone, but the second struck close enough to force **Hugo** back a step and nearly blinded a companion beside him. The Redcaps came on again as well, eager now that the frost slam had broken the defenders’ rhythm. One hacked at Darrak’s shield rim, another lunged toward Merrick, and a third tried to press through the gap forming near the slowed flank. Yet the party did not break. **Galborn**, though half-hampered by the cold, drew again and sent a second shaft into the ogre’s chest, this one landing with enough force to stagger it. **Darrak** seized that opening and crashed forward beneath the monster’s club, chopping hard into its leg. The blow bit deep enough to force a howl from the creature and make it list to one side.

To the right, **Haldir** called out a warning and moved to shore up the weakening edge. He struck at one of the Boggles trying to slip toward the ritual, driving it back into the dead roots, while **Cyr**, seeing the same opening, darted across the frost-slick stone with reckless speed and planted his short blade into the creature’s flank. **Xahlen** came in just behind him, turning from his earlier target long enough to thrust at a Redcap that had overextended itself against Merrick. The little killer twisted away from a mortal wound, but the combined pressure forced it back a pace. Merrick rose into that space with his mace already coming around, smashing the Redcap across the shoulder and shouting a prayer that was as much defiance as devotion. Even **Hugo**, who had retreated from the slime, recovered quickly enough to send a second burst of arcane force into the melee near the circle, breaking a Boggle’s rush and buying room for the defenders to close ranks once more.

By the end of those two rounds, the chamber had become a grinding knot of frost, roots, blood, and blue-white ritual light. The enemy had struck hard, and the ogre’s frost slam had nearly opened the path it wanted. But the line still held. **Cyr, Darrak, Galborn, Haldir, Hugo, Merrick,** and **Xahlen** were all in the fight now, each drawing part of the strain, each helping keep the battle from collapsing into the Septagram. Behind them the ritual continued, louder and brighter than before, while the portal still pulsed within its frame of living roots, promising that worse might yet come through if the defenders gave it the chance.

## The Ogre Falls

The Winter Court Ogre was already wounded when it tried to force its way through the defenders again. Frost still coated the cavern floor from its earlier slam, and every movement of its massive club scattered chips of ice across the stone. **Darrak** stayed in front of it, shield up and feet planted as firmly as the slick ground allowed, while **Galborn** shifted to give himself a clear line of fire. The ogre swung first, a broad murderous arc meant to clear the dwarf aside, but Darrak caught the blow on his shield and let the impact turn him rather than break him. As the creature overextended, he stepped inside the reach of the club and buried his weapon into the same damaged leg he had struck before.

The ogre lurched.

Galborn loosed at almost the same instant.

The arrow drove into the brute high in the chest, sinking deep enough to stop its roar halfway through. For a moment the creature stood there, huge and disbelieving, weight shifting from one ruined leg to the other. Darrak wrenched his weapon free and struck again, this time with everything he had left behind the blow. The ogre folded forward, its frozen club slipping from its hands and crashing against the stone. A heartbeat later the rest of the creature followed, hitting the cavern floor with enough force to shake dust from the ceiling.

“Down!” Darrak shouted.

Galborn was already turning toward the next threat.

The ogre’s death did not bring relief. It revealed how much of the battle had slipped past them while the front line had been occupied.

Several **Boggles** had worked their way around the defenders and were now inside the space everyone had been trying to protect. They moved with horrible fluidity, folding through gaps too narrow for ordinary creatures and skittering across the stone on long limbs slick with slime. One darted toward **Vandy**, forcing her back from the edge of the Septagram, while another lunged toward **Worliwynn** with both claws outstretched. **Sir Darrien** cut across the circle to intercept, driving his shield into the creature and knocking it sideways before it could reach her. “Inside the line!” he shouted. “They’re inside the line!”

That warning came almost too late for **Hugo**. A Boggle slipped around the outer edge of the Septagram and raked at him from the side, forcing him to twist away from the attack. Its claws tore cloth and nearly caught flesh before he could stumble backward. Xeril’s projected runes flickered across the floor as bodies crowded the ritual space, and Nystul snapped a sharp command without breaking his part of the chant. “Keep them off the circle!”

The defenders began collapsing inward to meet the breach. **Haldir** turned from the remnants of the front line and moved toward the ritualists, calling out to Worliwynn as he came. **Merrick** raised his mace and stepped between Vandy and one of the Boggles, forcing it to choose between continuing toward the circle or dealing with him first. **Xahlen** used the confusion to come at another from the side, striking quickly and then slipping away before its long arms could close around him. The fight had changed shape again. The defenders were no longer holding one clear front; they were trying to stop the battle from spilling directly across the glowing runes.

Only the **Redcaps** seemed delighted by the chaos.

Blood had been spilled across the chamber now—goblin blood, fey blood, and mortal blood—and the sight of it seemed to drive them into something close to ecstasy. One stamped through the remains of Haldir’s entangling roots, laughing as it dragged the edge of its sickle through a fallen body. Another turned toward the nearest wounded defender, but the third fixed on **Cyr**.

The halfling had just finished helping drive one of the Boggles back when he heard the heavy boots behind him. He turned in time to see the Redcap bearing down on him, yellow eyes bright with excitement and its crimson cap darkened by fresh blood.

“There you are,” it hissed.

Cyr barely got his weapon up before the sickle came down.

The impact drove him back a step. The Redcap pressed immediately, hacking again with none of the caution the goblinoids had shown. It wanted blood more than victory, and Cyr could see that clearly enough in its face. He ducked beneath the second swing, slashed at the creature’s arm, and tried to create space, but the Redcap followed him with a grin that only widened when its own blood began to run.

Across the chamber, Darrak saw what was happening but could not reach him without abandoning the gap left by the fallen ogre. Galborn had the same problem. The front had held, but the battle had already moved behind it.

The ogre was dead.

The danger was closer than ever.

## The Envoy of Winter

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/assets/monsters/M-CARD-Winter Court Envoy - Kaelith.webp"
    alt="Kaelith, an envoy of the Winter Court."
    caption="Kaelith, envoy of the Winter Court, steps through the portal."
    type="card-portrait"
>}}

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/assets/monsters/M-CARD-Unseelie Sprite.webp"
    alt="An armed Unseelie Sprite of the Winter Court."
    caption="Unseelie Sprites scatter above the battle as Kaelith advances."
    type="card-portrait"
>}}

The fall of the ogre should have bought the defenders a moment to breathe, but the portal changed before anyone could take advantage of it. The shifting blues and violets within the root arch darkened until they were almost black, and long streaks of shadow spread across the cavern floor beneath it. Frost followed, crawling over stone and dead roots in jagged patterns. The temperature dropped hard enough that every breath became visible, while the Boggles continued fighting near the Septagram and the Redcaps laughed amid the bloodshed as though the worsening battle were a festival arranged for their pleasure.

The small winged shapes came first. Four **Unseelie Sprites** shot through the opening and scattered overhead, little more than pale faces and beating wings glimpsed between patches of shadow. Their laughter was high and cruel, and sparks of fey magic danced around their hands as they circled above the defenders. **Galborn** turned his bow upward almost by instinct, but his attention shifted back toward the portal when a much taller figure stepped through behind them. Unlike the creatures that had preceded him, this newcomer did not rush into the battle. He walked.

The figure was tall and unnaturally gaunt, wrapped in a cloak that seemed woven from strips of darkness. Frost traced the edges of his clothing and clung to his shoulders like rime on winter branches. His eyes shone pale blue in the gloom, and at his side hung a thin rapier whose black blade seemed to swallow the nearby light rather than reflect it. He surveyed the cavern slowly—the fallen ogre, the tangled and dying goblinoids, the Redcaps fighting gleefully, the Boggles already past the defenders, and finally the burning Septagram at the far end of the chamber. None of it seemed to trouble him.

If anything, he looked amused.

One of the sprites settled briefly near his shoulder, whispering something into his ear. The newcomer smiled and stepped fully clear of the portal.

“So,” he said, his voice carrying easily through the noise of the battle, “these are the mortals who believe themselves capable of repairing what Winter has claimed.”

The fighting did not stop, but attention shifted toward him nonetheless. Even the Redcaps seemed to recognize that someone more dangerous had arrived. **Sir Darrien** glanced toward Nystul and then back to the newcomer, tightening his grip on his sword. **Worliwynn** drew closer to the Septagram, while **Haldir** looked from the stranger to the shadows gathering around him.

The fey lord—or whatever he was—showed no concern.

“You cut down servants and beasts and imagine that you have won something. You bring your little tokens of root and sea, bleed upon the stone, and call it balance.” His gaze moved toward Ethyra and Nystul. “You do not understand the thing you are touching.”

Nystul continued his part of the ritual without answering.

That seemed to irritate the stranger more than any threat might have.

His pale eyes settled on Ethyra next, then on the bloodied place where the heron had fallen. “And you,” he said. “You know better. Yet you would close the way anyway.”

Ethyra never stopped chanting.

The envoy's smile disappeared.

“You cannot cleanse the Knot,” he said, his voice suddenly colder. “Its corruption will stand in the name of the **Winter Court**.”

He drew the dark rapier.

Around him, the sprites shrieked with delight.

Then the smugness returned.

The fey raised one hand and turned it slowly enough for the defenders to see the three pale rings upon his fingers. They gleamed with a cold radiance like stars caught beneath ice. He touched one with his thumb and spoke a word too soft for anyone but the nearest sprites to hear.

His body blurred.

For an instant there were two of him.

Then three.

Then four.

Each stood in a slightly different posture, each holding the same shadow-black rapier, each wearing the same assured smile. They shifted around one another in a slow, fluid pattern until it became impossible to tell which figure had first stepped through the portal. **Galborn** lowered his bow a fraction as his target multiplied before his eyes, while **Darrak** swore under his breath.

The envoy noticed.

“Better,” he said. “Now perhaps this will be entertaining.”

The four Kaeliths moved forward together.

Above them, the sprites scattered toward the defenders, their tiny hands already gathering sparks of magic. One swept low across the chamber and marked **Darrak** in a ghostly radiance of its own *Faerie Fire*, leaving the dwarf outlined clearly for the other fey. Another darted toward Galborn, forcing him to turn aside as a tiny blade flashed past his face. Near the Septagram, the Boggles continued clawing at anyone they could reach, keeping **Merrick**, **Hugo**, **Xahlen**, and the others from simply concentrating on the new arrival.

**Cyr** still had his own problem. The Redcap attacking him lunged again, sickle whipping across at waist height. Cyr ducked beneath it and drove forward, trying to stay too close for the creature to use the weapon effectively, but the Redcap only laughed harder. Blood—its own and others’—covered its hands now, and the violence seemed to make it stronger rather than cautious.

Across the chamber, Kaelith watched all of it with obvious confidence. He did not hurry toward the party's warriors. He did not need to. His attention remained fixed upon the glowing lines surrounding Ethyra and Nystul.

Sir Darrien saw it.

“He isn't here for us,” he shouted. “He's going for the circle!”

Kaelith's four images smiled at exactly the same moment.

“Finally,” they said together, “someone understands.”

Shadow gathered around his feet.

## The Circle Under Attack

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/storm-tide/journals/007-journal/G-04-Ogre Down but boggles get close.webp"
    alt="The defenders fight near the ritual circle after the Winter Court Ogre falls."
    caption="With the ogre down, the surviving fey break through toward the ritual circle."
    type="wide"
>}}

Kaelith did not advance across the cavern so much as vanish from one place and emerge in another. Shadow folded around him, swallowing the four mirrored figures for an instant; then the darkness tore open beside **Sir Darrien** at the edge of the Septagram. One moment the knight had been watching the envoy from across the chamber, and the next Kaelith was within striking distance, his thin black rapier already coming forward. Darrien barely had time to turn. He abandoned any thought of attacking and dropped into a purely defensive posture, shield high and sword close, giving ground rather than allowing the fey to slip past him toward the ritualists.

Kaelith attacked with none of the heavy commitment of the ogre or the wild bloodlust of the Redcaps. His blade moved like a line of darkness drawn through the air, quick and precise, seeming almost too thin to parry. Darrien caught the first thrust on the edge of his shield and barely turned the second aside with his sword. The shadowy rapier hissed past his shoulder close enough that he felt its cold without being touched. “You stand very bravely for someone else’s ritual,” Kaelith said, almost conversationally. Darrien did not answer. His entire attention was fixed on keeping the envoy in front of him and away from the glowing lines only a few paces behind.

Across the chamber, **Galborn** saw the Unseelie Sprites circling above the melee and shifted his attention upward. He drew and loosed quickly, sending an arrow toward one of the darting fey. The sprite twisted in the air at the last possible moment, folding one wing and dropping beneath the shaft with effortless agility. Galborn fired again at another, only to see it snap sideways and let the missile pass through empty space. The tiny creatures laughed as they flew. They were not merely difficult targets because of their size; they seemed to read the path of an arrow almost as soon as it left the bow and move before it arrived.

The other sprites descended upon the defenders in quick, darting attacks. One swept past **Darrak**, its tiny weapon flashing before it climbed again beyond easy reach. Another harried **Haldir** as he tried to keep sight of both Kaelith and the ritual circle, while a third cut low toward **Hugo** and forced him to give ground rather than allow himself to be surrounded. The creatures never lingered. They struck, veered away, and climbed back into the air, leaving the party to turn constantly between threats at ground level and enemies overhead.

Near the remnants of the front line, **Cyr** was still locked in his brutal little duel with the Redcap. The creature pressed forward with its sickle, careless of defense now that blood covered the stone around them. Cyr avoided one swing, but the Redcap reversed the weapon faster than he expected and caught him with the return stroke. Pain flashed through his side as the blade drew blood. The Redcap froze for half a heartbeat, staring at the crimson on its sickle, and then its grin widened. The sodden cap upon its head darkened visibly, the red deepening until it seemed almost freshly dyed. Whatever strange vitality drove the creature responded to the bloodshed, and it gave a delighted laugh before coming after Cyr again.

The greatest danger, however, came from something far less imposing. All but one of the **Boggles** had been cut down or driven away from the ritual, but the last had slipped completely through the defenders. Ignoring the people around it, the creature crouched beside the Septagram and began clawing directly at one of the glowing lines. Its long fingers struck the rune-work once, twice, scraping slime across the light.

The Septagram flashed violently.

Every line in the seven-pointed star brightened at once, and **Nystul’s** chant faltered for the briefest instant before he forced the words onward. **Ethyra** never stopped. The light beneath the Boggle’s claws rippled like water struck by a stone but did not break. **Worliwynn** cried out a warning, while **Merrick**, **Xahlen**, and **Hugo** turned toward the creature almost together, realizing that the smallest surviving enemy had suddenly become one of the most dangerous things in the chamber.

“It’s holding!” Vandy shouted.

For the moment, she was right. The Boggle struck again, and the circle answered with another brilliant pulse, but the rune remained intact. Behind it, Darrien continued giving ground beneath Kaelith’s impossibly fast blade, Cyr fought to survive a Redcap growing more excited with every drop of blood, and the sprites wheeled overhead beyond Galborn’s arrows. The ritual was still alive, but the battle had finally reached the place the defenders had spent the entire fight trying to protect.

## The Binding and Renewal

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/storm-tide/journals/007-journal/G-05-Shadowy creature taunts and almost succedes but ritual is completed.webp"
    alt="The final struggle around the ritual circle as the healing of the Knot is completed."
    caption="Kaelith presses the attack as the ritual reaches its final moments."
    type="wide"
>}}

The Boggle struck the Septagram again, and for an instant the entire pattern seemed to buckle beneath the blow. Blue-white light raced through the seven points, flaring so brightly that everyone near the circle turned away. Then **Ethyra’s** voice rose above the battle, no longer competing with the noise of steel, shrieking fey, and grinding roots but somehow passing through all of it. She lifted both arms and spoke words older than Keoland, older perhaps than any kingdom still standing upon the Flanaess. **Nystul** joined her, his precise cadence winding around the archdruid’s chant like iron bands around living wood. The Septagram pulsed once, then again, and on the third beat every rune in the pattern blazed white.

The blood upon the circle began to shine. At first it seemed only to reflect the sudden brilliance, but then the light gathered above the place where the white heron had fallen. Silver radiance rose from the stone in long curling strands, taking shape as it climbed. Wings unfolded from the light, impossibly broad within the confines of the cavern, followed by the long neck and noble head of the same creature **Haldir** and **Galborn** had first encountered in the wetlands. The heron stood again, no longer flesh and blood but something vast and luminous, its feathers gleaming like moonlight upon still water. Haldir recognized it immediately. The bird that had freely given its feather, then willingly come to Wreckshore, had not simply been consumed by the ritual. Its sacrifice had become part of the healing.

The Spirit Heron opened its wings and gave a single cry. The sound was not loud in the ordinary sense, yet everyone felt it pass through the chamber, through bone and stone and into the Knot beneath them. The portal convulsed. Its shifting surface drew sharply inward, and the wind within the cavern reversed with enough force to whip cloaks, hair, ash, and loose debris toward the root arch. The **Unseelie Sprites** were the first to lose control. Their laughter turned to shrieks as their wings beat furiously against the pull, but one after another they were swept backward through the air and dragged into the portal like leaves caught in a sudden gale.

The last **Boggle**, still crouched over the Septagram, dug its claws into the stone and screamed as its long body stretched toward the opening. Slime streaked across the floor beneath it, but there was nothing it could hold. It was ripped away from the circle and pulled backward between the twisted roots. The **Redcaps** followed. Their delight in the bloodshed vanished as the force caught them; heavy boots scraped uselessly across the cavern floor while they hacked at anything within reach in a desperate effort to remain. Even the one fighting **Cyr** was torn away mid-swing, its sickle spinning from its hand as it disappeared into the swirling light. Within moments the lesser fey were gone, their screams fading somewhere beyond the threshold.

Only **Kaelith** remained. The pull struck him hard enough to tear the folds of his cloak backward, and the mirrored figures surrounding him began to distort. One vanished, then another, each reflection peeling away like smoke until only the true envoy stood beside **Sir Darrien**. Kaelith drove his shadow-black rapier into the stone and caught himself for a moment, his expression finally stripped of amusement. Darrien backed away rather than pursuing him, understanding that something far greater than steel had taken control of the chamber. Kaelith looked past the knight toward Ethyra, Nystul, and the radiant heron, then across the adventurers who had held the circle long enough for the ritual to succeed.

His composure returned before the portal claimed him.

“You have closed a door,” he called over the rising wind. “Do not mistake that for victory. **Winter remembers who barred its way.**”

The Spirit Heron cried again.

Kaelith’s rapier tore free of the floor. Shadow streamed from his cloak as he was dragged backward, first one step, then another, until even his magic could no longer hold him. He vanished through the portal with his eyes still fixed upon the defenders. The root arch shuddered violently, its living branches collapsing inward as the opening contracted to a narrow line of silver light and then disappeared altogether. The sudden wind died with it, leaving the chamber so quiet that the fading hum of the Septagram seemed loud by comparison.

The heron remained.

It passed once over the battlefield, its vast spectral wings sweeping through the places where slime, frost, and corruption still stained the stone. Wherever its light touched, those traces vanished beneath a wave of silvery-blue radiance. Frost melted. The Boggles’ slime evaporated. The unnatural pressure that had haunted the cavern since the Knot was first discovered lifted as though a great weight had been taken from the earth. Beneath their feet, the ley line no longer pulsed with the broken rhythm of corruption. It flowed evenly, deep and steady.

Only then did the defenders understand what they had actually protected.

They had not simply held a circle long enough for Ethyra and Nystul to complete a spell. They had given the wounded land enough time to answer for itself.

## Aftermath

The last echoes of the Spirit Heron’s cry faded into silence. The radiance that had filled the cavern softened, settling into the Septagram until the seven-pointed star no longer blazed but pulsed with a quiet, steady light, almost like a heartbeat beneath the stone. Frost melted from the cavern floor and ran in thin streams between the roots, while the last traces of Boggle slime and fey corruption vanished into silvery mist. The oppressive weight that had hung over Wreckshore for so long was simply gone. In its place remained the smells of blood, incense, wet earth, and ash.

**Ethyra** lowered her arms and sank to her knees, exhausted. For several moments she remained there with her head bowed, one hand pressed against the stone as if listening to something far beneath it. **Nystul** stood beside her, breathing heavily but otherwise composed. He surveyed the circle, the fallen creatures, and the place where the portal had vanished, then spoke a single word of power. The glowing lines of the Septagram dimmed in sequence until only a faint blue trace remained upon the floor.

No one rushed to speak.

**Worliwynn** approached Ethyra first and rested a hand upon her shoulder. **Vandy Banderdash** stood a few paces away, taking in everything she had witnessed with an expression that mixed wonder with the instinctive need to remember and record it accurately. **Sir Darrien** cleaned the dark residue from his blade and returned it to its sheath, though his attention remained on the place where Kaelith had disappeared. Around them, the adventurers slowly lowered weapons that had scarcely left their hands since the portal first opened.

For **Haldir** and **Galborn**, however, the silence carried something more. Their eyes went to the place where the white heron had fallen. Both had known the bird before it became part of the ritual. Haldir had spoken with it. Galborn had found its nesting grounds. Together they had carried its freely given feather back to Worliwynn. The sacrifice had been difficult to witness, and some among the party had recoiled from it, but what had happened afterward changed the meaning of the act. The heron had not simply died. Its spirit had risen from blood and light, driven the Winter Court from the cavern, and remained long enough for all of them to understand that something of it endured.

Worliwynn finally broke the silence.

“The Knot is quiet.”

Nystul glanced toward the dead root mass. “More than quiet.”

Ethyra lifted her head. Fatigue showed plainly in her face, but there was satisfaction there as well. “It is healed.”

That distinction mattered. Worliwynn’s earlier rite had suppressed the corruption and bought them time; this was something else. The broken current beneath Wreckshore no longer strained against its bindings. The unnatural whispers were gone, the fey influence had been driven out, and the power moving beneath the cavern felt deep and steady rather than sickened and erratic.

The adventurers had done what Ethyra had asked of them. They had not performed the great rite themselves, but without them it could never have been completed. **Cyr, Darrak, Galborn, Haldir, Hugo, Merrick, and Xahlen** had stood between the Septagram and the Winter Court while the ritual unfolded behind them. They had held against the goblinoids and Dark Hounds, survived the Redcaps and Boggles, brought down the Winter Court Ogre, and kept the circle intact long enough for Kaelith himself to fail.

Eventually the party began tending wounds and gathering scattered equipment. Vandy retrieved writing materials and began recording what she could while the details remained fresh. Nystul and Ethyra spoke quietly near the roots, comparing what each could sense beneath the cavern. Worliwynn remained close to the ritual site, occasionally touching the earth as if reassuring herself that the corruption truly had passed.

Kaelith’s final words still lingered.

*Winter remembers who barred its way.*

No one yet knew exactly what that promise meant. The portal was closed, the Knot was healed, and the creatures of the Winter Court had been driven from Wreckshore. But their envoy had not been destroyed. He had seen the adventurers clearly, and they had seen him.

For the first time, the conflict beneath Wreckshore had reached beyond a single corrupted place.

The Winter Court now knew their names.

## Hugo and Xeril

As the party made its way out of the cavern and back through the cabin above, **Hugo** heard the faint mechanical irregularity he had noticed when **Nystul** first arrived.

*Click… whirr… grind.*

Hugo mentioned it to Nystul and offered to fetch his tools and take a look at **Xeril**. The wizard was skeptical, but politely agreed. Once the little clockwork construct was brought down to the cabin table, Hugo opened one of its access plates and almost immediately found the problem: a tiny bent lever catching against the edge of its guide.

A slight adjustment was all it took.

The grinding stopped, Xeril returned to perfect silence, and Nystul regarded Hugo with considerably more interest than before.

It was a small moment after everything that had happened beneath Wreckshore, but Nystul would remember it.

## Epilogue: The River Beneath the World

The days following the ritual pass in a calm unlike anything Wreckshore has known since the adventurers first came there. The oppressive feeling that once clung to the cabin and the land around it is gone. No whispers creep through the darkness, no unseen presence seems to watch from beneath the earth, and the night is filled again with ordinary sounds—the wind moving through grass, the wash of the tide, and the calls of birds settling among the reeds.

Life returns quickly. Fishermen begin using the repaired camp again, and there is already talk of making greater use of **Merrick’s planned pier** once the work is finished. Smoke rises from the rebuilt smokehouses. The small shrine to **Obad-Hai** stands quietly against the wild land beyond the camp, while the shrine to **Osprem** faces the sea. Fishermen remark upon fuller nets, and birds return to stretches of shore they had abandoned. None of these changes are dramatic by themselves, but taken together they leave little doubt.

The corruption is gone.

Yet on the morning after the ritual, **Ethyra** asks the adventurers to return with her to the cavern beneath the cabin. **Nystul** accompanies them, his curiosity apparently having survived the previous night intact. The chamber is almost unrecognizable in its stillness. The great mass of roots remains, but the sickly pulse that once moved through them is absent. Ethyra walks to the place where the Septagram had been drawn, kneels, and presses her palm against the earth.

She remains that way for a long time.

Nystul watches without interrupting her. Eventually his expression changes as though he, too, has noticed something unexpected.

Ethyra slowly opens her eyes.

“It has moved.”

She rises and brushes the dust from her hand. “The Knot is no longer here.”

Nystul gives a thoughtful nod. “Yes. The flow has shifted. Redistributed, perhaps. Like a current diverted around an obstruction.”

Ethyra crouches again and traces a curving line through the dust with one finger. “Ley lines are not walls beneath the earth. They are rivers. They move beneath the skin of the Oerth, slowly perhaps, but they move nonetheless.” She draws several smaller lines joining the first and then wipes away the point where they meet. “A Knot forms where those currents gather. What we did here disturbed that meeting place.”

Haldir looks toward the dead roots. “Destroyed it?”

“No,” Ethyra answers. “Changed it.”

She studies the line she has drawn. “Imagine casting a great stone into a river. The water does not vanish. It passes around the stone and finds another course. Somewhere, eventually, those currents may gather again and another Knot may form.”

She looks around the cavern.

“But not here.”

There is satisfaction in Nystul’s expression now. He folds his hands behind his back and surveys the chamber as though reconsidering the entire affair. “Then you have accomplished something rather useful without intending to. Places where power gathers have an unfortunate tendency to attract people who want it.”

“Wizards,” Cyr says.

“Certainly.”

“Priests?” Merrick asks.

“Some.”

Darrak looks at him. “And the things you didn't mention?”

Nystul gives the dwarf a thin smile. “Usually the more troublesome category.”

Ethyra rises. “The Knot could have made Wreckshore a prize. Eventually others would have discovered it. Some would have wished to study it. Others to control it. Some would have cared very little what happened to the people living above it.” She gestures toward the silent roots. “Now there is nothing here to possess.”

For the fishermen and families trying to build something at Wreckshore, that may prove as important as cleansing the corruption itself.

## The Envoy

Later, when the group has returned aboveground, the conversation turns to the creatures that came through the portal. Ethyra listens to their questions before answering.

“They were not beasts wandering accidentally through an open door. They belonged to the **Winter Court**.”

She explains that the goblin-like warriors were not goblins at all, but fey shaped into something resembling them—lesser soldiers carrying a touch of winter within themselves. The **Redcaps** are different creatures entirely, drawn to violence and bloodshed rather than service or duty, while the **Boggles** are malicious tricksters whose talents allow them to go where defenders believe themselves secure.

“These were not an army,” Ethyra says.

Nystul makes a quiet sound of agreement. “A probing action.”

Ethyra nods. “A test. Something opened a path into our world and they wanted to know what stood on this side of it.”

Hugo glances toward the cabin. “And the one who came last?”

At that, both Ethyra and Nystul grow more serious.

“The envoy was Sidhe,” Ethyra says. “Such beings do not cross into our world casually. When they appear, there is purpose behind it.”

Nystul adds, “And unlike the creatures sent before him, he understood precisely what the ritual was doing.”

“He was not there to conquer Wreckshore,” Ethyra continues. “He needed only to prevent one moment from occurring—the completion of the rite. Had the circle failed, the way would have remained open and the corruption would have endured.”

Her gaze passes over the members of the party.

“He failed.”

There is no triumph in the way she says it.

“He is dangerous not merely because he carries powerful magic. He is dangerous because beings such as that possess patience. He will remember what happened here.”

Darrak grunts. “So we should expect him back?”

“Not here,” Ethyra replies. “The Knot has moved. The doorway he sought is gone. There is nothing beneath Wreckshore now that the Winter Court needs.”

Nystul nods. “From a strictly practical perspective, you have made the place quite uninteresting.”

“That does not mean **you** are uninteresting,” Ethyra says.

The words quiet the group.

“You opposed them. You saw their envoy. You prevented him from accomplishing what he came here to do.” She pauses. “You have stepped into their notice.”

The wind moves in from the water, carrying the clean smell of salt.

“So no,” Ethyra says. “You need not spend every night watching the shoreline for the Winter Court.”

Her expression hardens slightly.

“But I would not forget them.”

## The Guardian

At first light the following morning, Wreckshore lies beneath pale gold sunlight. The sea is almost perfectly calm, and the gentle surf moves against the beach with none of the unease that once seemed to hang over the place. From the porch of the cabin, the repaired camp looks almost ordinary.

Then someone notices the bird standing at the water’s edge.

A **white heron** waits in the shallows.

Haldir and Galborn recognize it immediately.

It stands exactly as the other had stood when Haldir approached it among the nesting grounds—the same long neck, the same patient stillness, the same watchful bearing. Yet the morning light catches strangely upon its feathers, and for brief moments they seem to shine from within rather than merely reflect the sun.

No one needs to explain what they are seeing.

Memory returns with it: the feather freely given, the heron walking willingly into the cavern, Ethyra kneeling beside it, and the blood upon the Septagram. What happened there remains difficult for some of them to reconcile. The sacrifice was real. So was the life that had been given.

But so was what followed.

The heron turns its head toward the cabin.

For a long moment it simply watches them.

Then it spreads its wings.

For an instant the white feathers become something more—vast, silver, almost translucent—and the shape they saw rising from the ritual circle seems to exist again beneath the morning sky. The vision lasts only a heartbeat. The heron lifts from the water, circles once over the beach, the rebuilt smokehouses, the two small shrines, and the cabin itself.

Then it settles again farther down the shore.

The Knot is gone. There is no ley-line nexus beneath Wreckshore for it to guard.

Its vigil is for something else now.

The waves roll in. The wind moves through the grass. Work will continue on the pier, fishing boats will come and go, and smoke will once again rise above the camp.

And from time to time, somewhere along the water’s edge, a white heron will be watching.

**Wreckshore is no longer alone.**
