# Fifty Years of Adventure

*From Original D&D to the Old School Renaissance*

**by Kam Tisk**

For more than fifty years, Dungeons & Dragons has changed through numerous editions, but the heart of the hobby remains remarkably familiar: friends gathering around a table to explore fantastic worlds and create stories together.

This week's Dispatch looks at the old-school style of play, how it differs from later editions, and why that approach continues to appeal to players today.

## A Hobby Unlike Any Other

Tabletop role-playing offers something novels and video games cannot fully reproduce: the freedom for players to attempt anything they can imagine. The referee brings the world to life, the dice introduce uncertainty, and the decisions made around the table create adventures unique to each group.

## For the Veterans

Players who began with First or Second Edition will recognize a world where exploration is dangerous, resources matter, and characters earn their reputations through survival, ingenuity, and a little luck.

## For Players of Third and Fourth Edition

Old-school play puts less emphasis on finding the correct skill, feat, or character ability. Players describe what they attempt, and the referee determines what happens. The emphasis shifts from what is written on the character sheet to what the player can imagine.

## For Players of Fifth Edition

Beginning adventurers are not superheroes. Combat can be dangerous, enemies do not exist simply to provide balanced encounters, and sometimes the smartest victory comes from avoiding a fight entirely.

## A Different Philosophy

The world exists independently of the player characters. The dungeon was not built for them. Monsters do not scale to their level. Sometimes the right answer is negotiation, retreat, or returning when the party is better prepared.

## More Than Rules

OERIK isn't about declaring one edition of Dungeons & Dragons superior to another. It celebrates a particular tradition of fantasy role-playing: exploration, danger, player ingenuity, and the classic World of Greyhawk.
