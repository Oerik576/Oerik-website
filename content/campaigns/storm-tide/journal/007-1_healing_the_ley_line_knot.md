## The Whispers Return

In the weeks following the discovery of the **Barrow of Nyzali**, Wreckshore had begun to change. What had once been little more than an abandoned fishing camp beneath the shadow of the Fangs was slowly becoming a place where people might live and work again. A shrine to **Obad-Hai** had been erected near the camp to protect the land from whatever lingering corruption the Chikleek had left behind. At **Brother Merrick’s** suggestion, plans were laid to expand the pier so fishing boats could once again moor there, the damaged smokehouses were rebuilt, and a small shrine to **Osprem** was dedicated near the water.

For a time, it appeared that the darkness beneath Wreckshore had finally passed.

Then **Cyr Baggins** began hearing the whispers again.

At first they were faint enough to dismiss—the suggestion of voices beneath the floorboards or somewhere beyond the cabin walls. But they persisted. When Cyr traveled into **Seaton’s Cove**, the voices disappeared entirely. When he returned to Wreckshore, they returned with him. Whatever he was hearing was not following Cyr.

It was waiting beneath the camp.

Cyr told the others, and the adventurers sent word to **Worliwynn**. The gnome druid returned to Wreckshore and descended with them into the old Chikleek lair beneath the cabin. The corruption they had encountered there before was no longer obvious, but the cavern still felt wrong. The great tangle of roots remained, and somewhere beneath the stone ran a faint vibration more felt than heard.

Worliwynn spent a long time examining the chamber. She knelt beside the roots, pressed her hands against the earth, and listened.

At last she stood.

“This place is a knot in the Oerth’s lifeblood,” she said. “A crossing of ley lines. Places such as this gather power naturally, but something has wounded this one.”

She looked toward the remains of the Chikleek lair.

“The creature you destroyed did not cause what happened here. It was drawn to it. Fed by it, perhaps. The Chikleek was only a symptom.”

That changed the nature of the problem immediately. The adventurers had believed they had destroyed the source of the corruption when they slew the creature beneath the cabin. Instead, they had removed only one manifestation of something much deeper.

Worliwynn placed her hand against the earth again.

“I can quiet it for a time. But I cannot heal it.”

She looked first to Haldir and then toward Hugo.

“To mend this completely will require both sides of the power flowing through this place—the living strength of the Oerth and the discipline of arcane magic. A druid and a wizard, each far more powerful than I.”

The whispers beneath Wreckshore had returned because the wound beneath it had never truly closed.

Now they knew where to begin.

## Worliwynn’s Plan

Worliwynn believed she could temporarily suppress the corrupted Knot, but the effort would require three offerings representing the natural forces around Wreckshore: **Root, Tide, and Sky**.

The first she already possessed—an acorn taken from an ancient oak grove and kept for rites of renewal. The second would have to come from the **Temple of Osprem** in Seaton’s Cove, something touched by the sea and consecrated to the goddess. The third was less certain. Worliwynn believed its nature would be revealed through divine guidance at **Stone Ring Pond**.

Once the Knot had been suppressed, there would be time to seek those capable of healing it permanently.

The party divided the work.

**Brother Merrick** would travel to the Temple of Osprem and ask for the Token of Tide. **Hugo** and **Cyr** would visit the **Hall of Illumination**, where **Vandy Banderdash** might know how to reach a wizard powerful enough to assist. **Haldir** and **Galborn** would accompany Worliwynn to Stone Ring Pond to seek the Token of Sky.

**Darrak** remained at Wreckshore, keeping watch over the camp and the cavern beneath it.

For the first time since the whispers had returned, they had something resembling a plan.

But it was only a plan to buy time.

## Merrick and the Token of Tide

Brother Merrick found the **Temple of Osprem** overlooking the harbor at Seaton’s Cove, its whitewashed stone bright against the gray-blue water beyond. The temple served sailors, fishermen, and those whose lives depended upon the moods of the sea. A basin of seawater stood near the entrance, and among the relics within was an old whale tooth polished smooth by generations of reverent hands.

Merrick was brought before **Selvara Tideborn**, the temple’s High Priestess. A former navigator before entering Osprem’s service, Selvara had the bearing of someone accustomed to storms, uncertain waters, and people who underestimated both. She listened without interruption while Merrick explained what the party had discovered beneath Wreckshore: the return of the whispers, Worliwynn’s examination of the cavern, and the corrupted ley-line Knot that lay beneath the fishing camp.

When he finished, Selvara studied him for a moment.

“You are asking for something consecrated to Osprem,” she said. “Not as a charm for safe passage or a blessing upon a vessel, but to place within a rite you do not yet fully understand.”

Merrick did not deny it. Worliwynn had been clear that the temporary suppression required a token of the sea, and without it the corruption beneath Wreckshore would continue to spread.

Selvara walked to the seawater basin and reached beneath its surface. When her hand emerged, she held a small shell, pale and smooth, marked with the signs of Osprem’s blessing.

“This has rested in consecrated water and been carried in rites for those who entrust themselves to the sea,” she said. “If I give it to you, I must know how it will be used.”

Merrick explained that the token would become one part of a threefold offering—Root, Tide, and Sky—used to quiet the wounded Knot until stronger hands could heal it permanently. The shell would not be kept, sold, or used for personal gain. It would be surrendered completely to the rite.

Selvara closed her fingers around the shell.

“Then swear it.”

She held it between them.

“By salt and storm, do you vow this gift will be used for balance, never for greed?”

Merrick placed his hand over his holy symbol.

“I swear it.”

Selvara watched him long enough that the silence itself seemed part of the test. Then she opened her hand and placed the shell into his palm.

“Then take it.”

The shell was cool despite having rested in the water, and for the briefest moment Merrick thought he could hear the distant movement of surf within it.

Selvara regarded him more gently now. “Whatever lies beneath Wreckshore, do not mistake a successful rite for mastery over it. The sea teaches that lesson often enough. Sometimes the best one can do is restore the course things were meant to follow.”

Merrick closed his hand carefully around the **Consecrated Seashell Token**.

“That is what we are trying to do.”

“Then may Osprem favor the attempt.”

With the Token of Tide secured, Merrick turned back toward Wreckshore. One of the three offerings Worliwynn required was now in their hands.

## Hugo, Cyr, and the Hall of Illumination

While Merrick went to the Temple of Osprem, **Hugo Baudelaire** and **Cyr Baggins** made their way to the **Hall of Illumination** in Seaton’s Cove. The white-stone building smelled of parchment, ink, and candlewax, and the quiet halls were lined with inscriptions in Suel and Old Oeridian. A copper statue of **Delleb**, god of reason and intellect, stood within the hall beneath the symbol of the golden flame.

They found **Vandy Banderdash** among the books and records. The High Librarian listened carefully as Hugo explained what Worliwynn had discovered beneath Wreckshore: a corrupted ley-line Knot, temporarily beyond their ability to heal, and the need for a wizard powerful enough to work alongside a great druid.

Cyr added the detail that had first brought the danger back to their attention.

“The whispers stop when I leave Wreckshore,” he told her. “In Seaton’s Cove, there’s nothing. But when I go back to the cabin, they start again.”

That caught Vandy’s attention immediately. The voices were not a lingering effect upon Cyr. They were tied to the place itself.

Hugo explained that Worliwynn could suppress the Knot for a time, but not repair it. The permanent rite would require arcane power well beyond Hugo’s own training. Vandy considered the problem in silence, turning over possible names and contacts.

Another voice interrupted before she could answer.

**Ichabod Trevors**, who had been listening nearby, stepped forward with an expression that made his opinion plain.

“You are considering Nystul.”

Vandy glanced toward him.

“I am considering everyone capable of the work.”

“Nystul is not merely a capable wizard,” Ichabod replied. “He meddles. And powerful eyes have a habit of attracting other powerful eyes.”

Hugo did not dismiss the warning, but neither did it change the problem before them. Whatever risks came with calling upon a wizard of such reputation, the corruption beneath Wreckshore was already active.

“We are not asking him to investigate a curiosity,” Hugo said. “We are trying to stop something that is already spreading.”

Cyr nodded. “And whatever is down there knows when I’m close enough to hear it.”

Vandy looked from one to the other. Their account left little room for delay.

At last she nodded.

“I believe you.”

Ichabod exhaled sharply, but Vandy continued before he could object again.

“I can send word through my contacts. Gradsul first, and from there farther if necessary. If Nystul can be reached, the message will find its way toward him.”

Hugo inclined his head. “That is all we can ask.”

“No,” Vandy said. “You are asking considerably more than that. You are asking a very powerful and very busy wizard to travel to a fishing camp in Salinmoor because something beneath the earth is whispering to a halfling.”

Cyr looked at her.

“When you say it that way, it does sound a little strange.”

For the first time, Vandy smiled.

“Fortunately, strange things are often the ones worth writing down.”

With that, she began preparing the message. Whether Nystul would answer was another question, but the request would be sent.

For now, that was enough.

## Haldir, Galborn, and the Token of Sky

While Merrick went to the Temple of Osprem and Hugo and Cyr sought help at the Hall of Illumination, **Haldir Sorenson** and **Galborn Valgwyn** accompanied **Worliwynn** to **Stone Ring Pond**. The ancient circle stood quiet beneath the open sky, twenty-four weathered stones surrounding the still water. Worliwynn entered the ring alone at first, knelt near the pond, and began to pray.

    type="card-landscape"

For a long while nothing happened.

Then the air changed.

The surface of the pond trembled though there was no wind, and Worliwynn slowly raised her head. When she finally spoke, her voice was quiet.

“A white heron,” she said. “A feather freely given, touched by the first dew of morning.”

That was the Token of Sky.

Finding the bird proved easier than convincing it to help. **Galborn** led them into the wetlands where he knew the herons nested, reading the soft ground and broken reeds until they found a quiet stretch of water sheltered by tall grass. Several birds moved among the shallows, but one stood apart from the others—a great white heron, tall and still, with the calm bearing of something that seemed to belong to the place as much as the water and reeds themselves.

Haldir stopped well short of it.

He whispered the words of **Speak with Animals**, and when the spell settled over him, the sounds of the marsh changed. The cries and calls of the birds became something more than noise.

Haldir approached slowly.

“The land is ill,” the heron said.

Haldir stopped.

“The water knows it. The reeds know it. The birds know it.”

“We know it too,” Haldir replied. “There is a wound beneath Wreckshore. We mean to heal it.”

The heron watched him with one dark, steady eye.

“And what do you ask of me?”

“A feather,” Haldir said. “Freely given. We need it for the rite.”

The bird remained motionless for several breaths.

Then it lifted one wing.

A single white feather loosened and drifted downward.

“Then carry the sky with you.”

Haldir caught the feather before it touched the mud.

Galborn, watching nearby, said nothing. Neither did Worliwynn. There was something about the exchange that made silence seem more fitting than celebration.

The feather alone was not enough. Worliwynn’s vision had shown it touched by the first dew of morning, so they remained in the wetlands through the night. The hours passed quietly beneath the open sky, and before dawn they prepared the feather for the final part of the rite.

As the first light appeared over the horizon, beads of dew formed across the grasses and reeds. Haldir carefully drew the white feather through the fresh moisture until droplets clung to its barbs.

The Token of Sky was complete.

Before they left, Haldir looked back toward the water.

The same white heron still stood in the shallows, watching them go.

## The Temporary Suppression

By the time the others returned to Wreckshore, **Darrak** had kept watch over the camp and the cavern beneath it while the three parts of Worliwynn’s plan were gathered. Merrick brought the **Consecrated Seashell Token** from the Temple of Osprem. Haldir and Galborn returned with the white heron’s feather, touched with the first dew of morning. Worliwynn already carried the acorn from the ancient oak grove.

Root. Tide. Sky.

With the three offerings assembled, Worliwynn led the party back beneath the cabin and into the old Chikleek lair. The corrupted Knot still pulsed somewhere beneath the mass of dead and twisted roots, and the whispers seemed stronger now that they had returned with the means to oppose it.

Worliwynn chose a place near the root mass and began laying out the rite. She marked a protective circle upon the stone with pieces of driftwood and small stones, adding simple druidic signs at intervals along its edge. At the center she placed the three offerings: the oak acorn for Root, the consecrated shell for Tide, and the dew-touched feather for Sky.

The others stood back while she knelt before them.

Her chant began softly.

At first nothing changed. Then the roots along the cavern wall tightened. Dry branches creaked against one another, and the vibration beneath the floor grew stronger. The whispers rose with it, overlapping until individual words were impossible to distinguish. Whatever lay within the Knot seemed to recognize that something was being done to it.

Worliwynn did not stop.

She touched the acorn first and pressed it against the stone. The smell of damp earth filled the chamber. She moved to the shell, and for an instant the distant sound of surf seemed to pass through the cavern. Last came the white feather. As her fingers closed around it, the air stirred though there was no wind belowground.

The roots convulsed.

Several snapped taut as though something beneath them had pulled hard from the other side. The whispers sharpened, filling the chamber with a sudden pressure that made the walls seem closer. Darrak tightened his grip on his weapon while the others watched the circle, but Worliwynn remained focused upon the rite.

Her voice rose.

The three tokens answered together.

A faint pulse moved outward from the circle and through the cavern floor. The acorn darkened against the stone, the shell gleamed wetly, and the feather lifted for a heartbeat before settling again. Then the pressure beneath the chamber broke.

The whispers stopped.

Not gradually. Completely.

The sudden silence was so abrupt that for several moments no one moved. The deep vibration beneath their feet faded with it, and the sickened tension that had lingered in the cavern since their first descent loosened its hold.

Worliwynn remained kneeling, breathing heavily.

At last she opened her eyes.

“It holds.”

Haldir looked toward the root mass. “It’s healed?”

Worliwynn shook her head.

“No. Quieted.”

She gathered herself and rose slowly. “The wound remains. I have only pressed it closed for a time.”

The distinction was clear enough. The corruption was no longer actively bleeding into Wreckshore, but nothing had been permanently repaired.

Still, the silence mattered.

For the first time since Cyr had begun hearing the voices again, the Knot beneath Wreckshore was still.

They had bought themselves time.
