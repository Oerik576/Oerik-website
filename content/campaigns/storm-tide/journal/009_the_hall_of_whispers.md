**Session 9 --- September 19, 2026**

**Adventurers:** Cyr Baggins, Darrak Goldenforge, Galborn Valgwyn, Haldir Sorenson, Hugo Baudelaire, Xahlen Thistlewacker, Brother Merrick\
**Adventure:** *The Hall of Whispers*

## Leaving the Black Fang Warren

The fighting was over, but nobody was ready to call the matter finished. In the ruined shrine beneath Oldstairs, the remains of the Black Fang cult surrounded the adventurers: the shattered idol, dead ratlings, blood, bones, crude fetishes, and the lingering stink of their rites. Somewhere beyond the chamber lay the strange archway they had found earlier, and somewhere in the darkness was the shadow-wrapped figure that had stood behind the idol before simply disappearing. The party discussed whether to continue exploring, but they had another responsibility with them. **Harl Netbinder** had survived his captivity, and after everything he had endured he wanted nothing more than to go home.

The deeper complex could wait. The adventurers had wounds to tend, a rescued man to return, and authorities in Seaton's Cove who needed to know what had been living beneath their streets. More importantly, nobody knew whether the ratlings they had killed represented the whole infestation. The shadowed figure had escaped, and other passages could still connect the warren with the town above. Whatever lay beyond the whispering arch had apparently waited for centuries. It could wait one more night.

Rather than retracing their route to the well, they followed another well-used passage upward. The tunnel emerged through a heavily overgrown opening on the north side of Oldstairs, near the fields and lanes of Green Hollow. **Galborn** studied the ground and vegetation. Animals and rats had used the area, and faint trails led toward the outskirts of town, but the entrance itself was surprisingly difficult to see unless someone already knew where to look. **Cyr** and **Xahlen** arranged a harmless tripwire and scattered powder where anyone entering or leaving would disturb it. They deliberately avoided anything dangerous. A farmer or curious child stumbling across the opening was more likely than an enemy conveniently stepping into a lethal trap.

## Harl Comes Home

Harl's first concern was his wife, **Lurla**. She had spent weeks believing that something had happened to him along Saltwharf, and by now much of the ward knew he was missing. Cyr volunteered to take him home. There was a practical reason to make sure a recently rescued prisoner reached his door safely, but Cyr also understood Saltwharf well enough to recognize another benefit. Lurla would see who brought her husband back, and word of Harl's rescue would spread quickly.

Cyr escorted Harl back into Saltwharf. Whatever private words passed between husband and wife mattered less than the simple fact that Harl had returned alive. Lurla had spent weeks asking questions about his disappearance. Now the missing man had walked home beside one of the adventurers who had gone beneath the town looking for the source of Saltwharf's troubles.

Harl's return also ended any realistic chance of keeping the tunnels quiet. Once he began telling people where he had been and what had taken him, curiosity would follow. While Cyr saw Harl home, **Darrak** went to the garrison and reported the hidden entrance and the fighting beneath Oldstairs to **Captain Lardus Longsaddle**. The danger might not be completely gone, and Longsaddle could at least put guards where they were needed.

## Counsel in the Night

**Brother Merrick** and **Haldir** went to the Temple of Osprem. The temple had closed for the night, and the guard initially had little interest in admitting unexpected visitors. Merrick persisted. Their wounds and battered appearance after the fighting beneath Oldstairs did much of the arguing for him, and before long they were brought before **Selvara Tideborn**, high priestess of Osprem.

Merrick described the ratling shrine built within a much older structure, the blood and bones, soot and fetishes, the strange whispers, and the mysterious shadow-covered figure that escaped during the battle. Selvara turned to Haldir for another judgment. As a druid, had he felt evil in the older place itself? Haldir did not think so. The original structure had not struck him as corrupt. The wrongness belonged to what the ratlings had done there.

Selvara returned to the fetishes stretched across the ancient archway. "Were they keeping something out," she asked, "or keeping something in?" Haldir recalled the dust and grime upon them. They had apparently hung undisturbed for a long time and did not look like something the ratlings regularly moved aside. If they were a ward, the ratlings themselves might have feared what lay beyond. When Selvara learned that Harl had already gone home, she understood that rumors would spread by morning. She sent word to the garrison that the entrances beneath Oldstairs should be watched and told Merrick and Haldir to be at the garrison the next morning.

Elsewhere, **Hugo** went to the **Hall of Illumination**, where **Vandy Banderdash** could make better use of the symbols and carvings he remembered from below. Their search began with the ratlings. The name **Raxivort** emerged from the books, a power associated with rats, bats, and the blue-skinned humanoids known as **xvarts**, with older references also connecting him to **Graz'zt**. The strange blue-hand imagery was less easily explained.

The older carvings were another matter. Once Vandy separated Hugo's description of the original stonework from the symbols the ratlings had added, she recognized the religious language of **Rao**, the ancient Flan god of peace, reason, and serenity. By the time they finished, the shape of the discovery had changed. The ratlings had not built a temple beneath Oldstairs. They had desecrated one.

## The Morning Council

The next morning brought the adventurers, clergy, scholars, and town authorities together at the garrison. **Mayor Oseph Menhemes** attended with his retainer, Selvara arrived with clergy and initiates from the Temple of Osprem, Vandy came from the Hall of Illumination, and Captain Longsaddle had guards ready to secure the entrances. Longsaddle asked Darrak to tell the mayor what had happened beneath the town.

Oseph listened through the description of the warren, the captives, the Black Fang shrine, and the battle. The idea that a colony of ratlings had established itself beneath Seaton's Cove plainly did not improve his morning.

"Rats! Rats beneath my town."

Then he asked the question that mattered most. "Do you think you got them all?"

No one could promise him that they had. Some ratlings might have escaped through passages the party never found. Worse, there was still the figure from the shrine. Hugo had the clearest recollection, and with Vandy's help he reconstructed what he had seen. The stranger had been roughly human-sized, perhaps a little slight, but its features were impossible to make out. Shadow did not merely fall across it. The figure seemed wrapped in darkness. It had stood behind the Black Fang idol during the battle and then stepped behind it and disappeared. The party had searched afterward and found no hidden door or passage explaining where it went.

Selvara had no intention of waiting for every question to be answered. Her people were ready to go underground. She made one thing clear before they left: this was a cleansing mission, not an expedition into every unexplored passage beneath Oldstairs. Longsaddle agreed. His guards would secure the approaches and accompany the clergy, but they were town soldiers, not dungeon explorers. Selvara and **Sister Carenna** tended the party's remaining wounds before the group set out.

## Back Beneath Oldstairs

The expedition returned through the concealed northern entrance. Cyr and Xahlen inspected the telltales they had left behind. The tripwire remained undisturbed. The powder showed many tracks, but they belonged to ordinary rats. In passages beneath Seaton's Cove, that was hardly surprising.

In better light, with the immediate danger gone, the Black Fang chamber looked different. Ratling refuse and ritual debris had obscured much of the original stonework. Once bodies and broken furnishings were moved aside, older carvings became easier to see beneath the soot, blood, and crude symbols. Vandy's conclusion from the previous night was confirmed. This had once been a sanctuary of Rao.

Selvara put her people to work. Ratling corpses were carried out. Bones, bloodied cloth, sacrificial remains, fetishes, broken pieces of the idol, and the accumulated filth of the cult were gathered and removed. Brother Merrick joined the acolytes. Water and rags exposed stone that had not been properly cleaned in years. Then the religious cleansing began. Blessed seawater ran across the floor and carved stone. Selvara scattered sea salt while prayers were spoken. Candles and incense replaced the ratlings' foul ritual fires, and brass bells sounded while the clergy of Osprem worked around the sanctuary, not claiming it for their goddess but stripping away what the Black Fang had imposed upon it.

As the rite continued, the whispers changed. The voices that had previously seemed confined to the older chamber beyond began to bleed into the sanctuary. They remained faint and indistinct, overlapping one another in fragments, but there was no mistaking them. When the final bell faded, the smell of blood and rot had given way to seawater and incense. The Black Fang markings were gone, but the older carvings remained. Selvara had cleansed the sanctuary. She had not reconsecrated it to Osprem. What remained belonged to **Rao**.

## Beyond the Arch

Attention returned to the archway the ratlings had covered in bones, claws, rat skulls, and knotted charms. Vandy examined the hanging fetishes carefully and found no real magic in them. Whatever the ratlings had believed, their barrier was superstition rather than enchantment. The fetishes came down, the stone was cleaned, and Vandy studied the inscription revealed beneath the grime.

**Chamber of Remembrance of the Old Gods.**

Selvara found no evil beyond the arch. No conventional magical aura announced itself either, although nobody was willing to equate "not detected" with "ordinary." Darrak and Xahlen examined the construction and reached another important conclusion. The stone beyond the arch was older than the Raoan sanctuary. The temple had been built around something already here.

Galborn studied the floor. The ratlings did not appear to have regularly crossed the threshold. There was evidence that something had passed this way before, but not recently. The signs were too old to tell whether they had been made one year ago or several. Cyr took the lead as they moved forward, keeping ahead of the others and watching the floor, walls, and stonework for traps.

## The Chamber of Remembrance

The passage opened into a chamber unlike the ratling warren behind them. A clear basin occupied its center, surrounded by ten small rune-marked stone pillars or pedestals. Narrow channels ran toward the water. Beneath its surface rested a carved stone fitted into an indentation, although it sat slightly askew, as though it had once belonged in a more precise position. The whispers were everywhere.

Vandy began working through the symbols. Some represented concepts rather than simple words. **Time** could be recognized. So could **Name**. Other markings suggested senses, place, truth, spirit, watching, speaking, and remembrance. Alcoves around the walls held personal objects. None looked like treasure placed there for safekeeping. They were keepsakes, the sort of possessions whose value depended upon who had owned them and why. Despite their age, they were remarkably well preserved.

A set of old prayer beads drew the party's attention. Touching them did nothing. They could not be lifted from their resting place. The adventurers experimented cautiously until, while explaining the attempt to Vandy, someone finally said aloud what they wanted.

"I wanted to hear the story of those prayer beads."

A young woman's voice answered.

"These beads are for the remembrance of my dear departed mother..."

The voice continued with memories of her mother. No ghost appeared. The beads did not move. Yet words spoken centuries before filled the chamber as clearly as conversation. The central stone suggested another function as well: **tell what should be remembered**. The Chamber had once been both archive and repository. People had come here to ask for memories, but they had also come to leave them.

## The Stone Ring Pond

**Worliwynn** was among the first to test the discovery deliberately.

"Tell me of the Stone Ring Pond."

The Chamber answered with more than one voice. One remembered a wedding at the stones. Another spoke of a religious ceremony. These were not explanations written by scholars. They were moments preserved by people who had been there. Worliwynn knew the stones at the pond were at least five centuries old, and she knew the place possessed unusual qualities of its own, particularly in the way memory and impressions could become focused there. What they were hearing beneath Oldstairs felt related.

Nothing inside the Chamber itself identified it as Raoan. The temple outside was Rao's, but this place was older. Selvara finally gave Vandy a slight smile.

"Can I ask a question now?"

"Be my guest."

"Tell me of the Temple of Rao."

The Chamber answered with memories of people discovering the older Chamber and later raising the sanctuary of Rao to protect it and use its knowledge for the good of the people. The temple had not created the Chamber. It had been built as its guardian.

## A Day Among the Memories

Once the party understood how to ask, the rest of the day became a conversation with the past. Vandy settled into the work with the concentration of a scholar who knew she had stumbled upon something that might occupy the Hall of Illumination for years. The oldest voices spoke in forms of Flan that few living people could follow. Later memories sometimes used Keoish. The answers also revealed the Chamber's most important limitation. It did not speak with the authority of an oracle. It **remembered**.

Two people could recall the same event differently. A later storyteller could embellish an older tradition. A frightened sailor might preserve superstition beside the observations of a priest or scholar. When Vandy compared some answers with surviving written histories, details did not always agree. The Chamber preserved what people had experienced, understood, and believed.

There was another boundary. Questions about the Black Fang ratlings produced nothing useful. Whatever process had once added new memories to the Chamber had ceased long before they arrived. The surviving archive reached no later than **476 CY**. It could tell the party about the distant past, but it knew nothing of the last century.

For the adventurers, that still left an extraordinary amount to ask.

## Keeping the Chamber Safe

Captain Longsaddle did not share the scholars' desire to spend the entire day listening to ancient voices. Once he was satisfied that the immediate site was secure and his guards understood their duties, he returned to the responsibilities waiting above. He left men behind to control access to the tunnels.

Selvara's initiates continued removing the remains of the Black Fang occupation. When the growing pile of ratling refuse and ritual debris threatened to become a problem of its own, Longsaddle made it clear that nobody was simply dumping it into a street above. The material was moved away from the sacred areas and toward spaces where it could later be properly removed or destroyed.

**Xahlen** offered money toward hiring laborers for the cleanup. He wanted the people who eventually controlled access to the ruins to remember that the party had not simply discovered the Chamber and walked away. They had helped recover it, clean it, and protect it. Xahlen also reached out through his uncle and other contacts to establish the party's connection to the site.

For the immediate future, the Temple of Osprem and the Hall of Illumination would oversee the discovery. Neither intended to claim the Temple of Rao. Selvara had cleansed it because it had been desecrated, not because she wanted to turn it into a sanctuary of Osprem. Rao still had worshippers elsewhere in the country, and news of an ancient Raoan temple beneath Seaton's Cove would eventually reach them. When that happened, a priest of Rao might have different ideas about who should be allowed into the Chamber.

For now, the Protectors of the Cove were the people who had found it.

## A Problem Solved, and Another Discovered

By the time the adventurers returned to **Mayor Oseph Menhemes**, the situation beneath Oldstairs looked very different from the one they had reported that morning. The Black Fang cult was broken. Harl Farshaw was home. The desecrated sanctuary had been cleansed, guards controlled the known approaches, and the dangerous-looking fetishes across the arch had proven to be little more than ratling superstition.

But beneath all of that they had discovered something nobody in Seaton's Cove knew existed. The Chamber of Remembrance was older than the Temple of Rao, older than much of the town above it, and filled with voices reaching back into the Flan past. It was not an oracle and it was not infallible, but it preserved memories that had vanished from every surviving book in the Hall of Illumination.

Oseph was relieved that the immediate danger had been contained. **Brewfest was only a few weeks away**, and Seaton's Cove would soon be filling with farmers, sailors, merchants, travelers, and visitors. An active ratling infestation beneath the streets would have been bad enough. Unsecured entrances leading curious festival-goers into ancient ruins would have been worse.

The mayor regarded the **500 gp reward** as money well spent. He would have local artisans seal entrances that did not need to remain open and establish controlled access through the others. The adventurers would still be able to return, but Oseph warned them not to assume every guard posted beneath Oldstairs would recognize them on sight. Captain Longsaddle could arrange access when they needed it.

That evening, the adventurers left the matter in the hands of the town, the clergy, and Vandy's scholars. They had gone beneath Oldstairs to destroy the Black Fang and rescue those the ratlings had taken. Instead they had uncovered a piece of Seaton's Cove that had been waiting beneath their feet for centuries. The ratlings had called the place dangerous and hung their charms across its door. The people who built it had called it a place of remembrance, and now, after centuries of silence, Seaton's Cove could hear its voices again.
