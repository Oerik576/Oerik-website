### An Old Friend Arrives

Cyr remained at the Salty Net with the little brass bell in his hand, turning it slowly between his fingers as the ordinary noise of Saltwharf carried on around him. Somewhere beneath Seaton’s Cove was a ratling that had escaped him, and somewhere beyond that mystery walked the strange two-tailed cat that had watched the fight beneath the wharf. He was still considering which trail to follow when the door opened and Darrak Goldenforge walked in.

The dwarf spotted Cyr and made his way over. “Heard you had some trouble down here,” Darrak said as he pulled out a chair. “Friend of mine in the guard told me about some rat-creature you dragged into the watch post. Heard a few other versions since then, too.” He gave Cyr a knowing look. “None of them sat right with me. Figured if something happened in Saltwharf, you’d know what actually happened.”

Cyr told him. He described the disappearing cats, the marked rat skull Tellen had found, and the trail beneath the wharf. He explained how he had discovered two strange rat-creatures trapping cats, killed one, and watched the other escape through a narrow drainage passage leading beneath the town. He also told Darrak about the large two-tailed feline that had watched the fight from the shadows and left behind the broken collar and bell.

Darrak listened without interrupting. When Cyr finished, the dwarf sat back and considered it for a moment. “Where there’s one rat, there’s a den of rats,” he finally said. “And if that one ran somewhere, I’d wager that’s where it went.” He glanced toward the floorboards. “The guard can’t go digging through every drain, cellar, and hole beneath town. Better we find where these things are hiding and deal with them ourselves.”

It was then that Cyr noticed Tellen Farshaw nearby. The boy had positioned himself where he could continue whatever work he was supposedly doing while remaining conveniently within earshot. He was trying very hard not to look as though he were listening, but the hopeful expression on his face betrayed him. Darrak noticed Cyr looking and followed his gaze toward Tellen before turning back.

“What do you think?” the dwarf asked.

Cyr looked down at the dented bell once more. A short while ago he had been wondering which trail to follow first. Now one of those trails had become considerably clearer. He nodded. They would find where the escaped ratling had gone and learn what was hiding beneath Seaton’s Cove.

“Good,” Darrak said, pushing himself away from the table. “Get your gear. We’ll meet back here tomorrow.”

Darrak headed back toward Wreckshore to gather the rest of the party. Cyr remained at the Salty Net, while nearby Tellen Farshaw made a poor show of returning his attention to his work.

### The Ratling Trail

The next morning, the rest of the party arrived at the Salty Net ready to investigate. Cyr led them back through Saltwharf to the place beneath the wharf where he had fought the two ratlings. The tide and the constant traffic of the waterfront had already begun to disturb whatever tracks the fleeing creature might have left, but Cyr was accustomed to reading a city differently. Years spent watching streets, alleys, rooftops, and escape routes had taught him that anyone trying to move unseen still left signs behind. The same, it seemed, was true of ratlings.

Near the pilings, Cyr noticed scratches cut into the wood. At first they looked like ordinary damage, one more set of scars among countless marks left by ropes, hooks, cargo, and weather, but their placement was wrong. Similar marks appeared farther along, scratched low into another post where they would be easily overlooked. Cyr began searching deliberately and found more: marks on the corners of crates, beneath loading platforms, and along the timbers bordering narrow passages between buildings. Some were partly hidden beneath grime or positioned where something small moving close to the ground would notice them more readily than a passing dockworker.

“They’re signs,” Cyr said after examining another set. He could not necessarily read each mark as a word, but the pattern was becoming clear. The ratlings had been marking their movements through Saltwharf, identifying paths through the district that allowed them to travel without drawing attention.

Once Cyr knew what to look for, the trail became easier to follow. He led the others away from the waterfront, stopping whenever another inconspicuous scratch appeared on wood or stone. The signs avoided the broadest streets and busiest crossings, instead leading through narrow lanes, behind warehouses, alongside old walls, and through places where small creatures could pass unnoticed. Gradually the smells of fish, tar, and salt water faded behind them as the buildings grew older and the streets more cramped.

The ratling trail was leading them into **Oldstairs**.

### The Forgotten Cistern

The trail carried the party deeper into Oldstairs, eventually leaving the busier lanes behind and bringing them to an abandoned orchard. Whatever purpose the ground had once served, it had long since been neglected. Cyr's trail had brought them this far, but among the weeds and old growth there was no obvious entrance to a ratling den. Haldir crouched beside Bendave and let the wolf take the scent from the ratling bell. After a moment, Bendave began working his way through the overgrown ground until he stopped at a rotting wooden cover almost hidden beneath the vegetation.

Beneath it was an old cistern.

The party pulled enough of the covering aside to examine the shaft. Looking down, they could make out water far below, perhaps fifty feet beneath them. If the ratlings had been using the cistern as a route into their warren, it was not an inviting one. There was some discussion of waiting until nightfall before going down, but that raised another problem. Rats were creatures of darkness, and whatever advantage the party might gain by approaching Oldstairs after sunset could just as easily favor the things they were hunting. They decided to make the descent while daylight remained above them.

As they prepared their ropes, movement nearby caught Cyr's attention. The large two-tailed feline was watching them again, perched in one of the orchard trees. Before anyone could approach her, the cat descended and made her way to the cistern. She slipped over the edge with the same unnatural confidence Cyr had seen beneath the wharf and vanished into the darkness below. The party waited for the splash that should have followed.

None came.

That settled any remaining question about whether the cistern led somewhere. The party secured their ropes and checked their equipment before beginning the descent. Cyr volunteered to go first. He swung himself over the edge and started downward, leaving the daylight of Oldstairs above him as he followed the two-tailed cat into the darkness beneath the town.

### Into the Ratling Warren

Cyr descended the long stone shaft until his boots reached the cold water at the bottom. The others followed one at a time, and once everyone was down they found an opening in the cistern wall just above the waterline. The two-tailed feline was already there, waiting silently inside. Beyond her, a narrow earthen passage sloped farther downward, its walls damp with runoff and patches of greenish lichen providing just enough light to distinguish stone from shadow.

It did not take long to find evidence that the passage was being defended. Threads of rope stretched across the tunnel with fragments of bone and broken pottery dangling from them, accompanied by crude arrangements of twine and feathers. Xalen carefully disarmed the warning line before the party continued. The precaution paid off when the passage opened into a low chamber where ancient wooden supports projected from the walls and broken crates, tiles, gnawed cloth, and other refuse littered the floor.

Two ratlings occupied the chamber along with a pair of unusually large rats. Because Xalen had disabled the alarm, none of them knew the party was coming. The adventurers struck before the sentries could react. Xalen opened the attack with an arrow, Haldir hurled his magical spear into one of the guards, and Xalen's badger charged into the chamber. The surprise was devastating. Both ratlings were brought down before they could raise an alarm.

The giant rats proved more troublesome. The narrow entrance prevented the party from bringing its full strength into the chamber at once, and the animals rushed the front ranks while everyone tried to maneuver through the bottleneck. Galborn stepped into the chamber and killed one of the creatures with a powerful series of blows, while Xalen and his badger engaged the remaining rat.

Once the chamber was secured, the party continued into the passages beyond. The tunnels twisted deeper through packed earth reinforced with scraps of wood and bone, their walls scratched with crude glyphs and signs of heavy traffic. Rats disappeared into side holes and narrow burrows as the adventurers approached.

Whatever Cyr had uncovered beneath the wharf was much larger than he had first suspected. These were not a handful of creatures hiding in a drain beneath Saltwharf. The tunnels beneath Oldstairs were a **warren**, and the party had now breached its outer defenses.

### The Spirit Watchman

Beyond the first guard chamber, the party discovered a cramped passage leading away from the active ratling tunnels. It opened into the remains of a much older underground structure. Broken masonry and fallen timbers filled the chamber, and an ancient stairway that had once led upward was completely buried beneath the collapse. Darrak examined the stonework and could tell that this was not simply another part of the ratling warren. The chamber had been built underground long before the ratlings began digging beneath Oldstairs.

Near the far wall sat a motionless figure pinned beneath a heavy timber.

At first it appeared to be another ancient corpse. Its clothing resembled the uniform of a guardsman or watchman, although the style was unlike anything currently worn in Seaton's Cove. Whatever authority the man had once served, it belonged to an earlier age. Then the figure slowly raised its head and looked at them.

Weapons came ready, but the watchman made no attempt to attack. He did not struggle against the timber or reach toward the party. He simply watched them. That prompted an immediate debate. Some wondered whether they should destroy the apparent undead creature before it became dangerous, while others pointed out that its behavior was nothing like the mindless undead they had encountered before.

Beside the watchman rested an old bronze lantern. Its flame burned with a steady pale blue-white light but produced neither smoke nor noticeable heat, and there was no visible source of fuel. The lantern was close to the trapped figure, yet apparently just beyond his reach. Whatever had happened in this chamber, both watchman and lantern had endured it together for centuries.

Brother Merrick was brought forward. The party discussed whether there might be some means of speaking with the dead, but the watchman's silence did not seem threatening. Merrick instead considered the possibility that the spirit was bound to the place by something left unfinished. He began a respectful funerary rite, offering prayers and blessings for the dead rather than attempting to drive the spirit away by force.

As Merrick's prayer echoed through the ruined chamber, the pale flame of the lantern brightened. Cool blue-white light spread across the old masonry, and the watchman slowly raised himself as far as the fallen timber allowed. For the first time, the weariness seemed to leave his ancient features. He reached toward the lantern and placed a translucent hand upon it.

The watchman's form dissolved into drifting motes of pale light.

They crossed the small distance that had separated him from the lantern and disappeared into its flame. When the last of them vanished, nothing remained beneath the fallen timber but the remnants of a man who had kept his vigil far longer than any mortal watchman should have been asked to endure.

The lantern remained.

Examining it more closely, the party found an ancient bronze hooded lamp bearing the faded crest of old Seaton's Cove. Its flame could not be extinguished by wind, water, or ordinary means while carried through the Underfold. More than light accompanied it there: companions remaining close to the lantern felt steadier against supernatural fear and influences that sought to control the mind. Whatever power sustained those protections seemed tied to the buried world beneath the town; outside the Underfold, only the lantern's ever-burning flame remained.

Darrak looked again at the chamber around them. The ancient watch post had been built underground from the beginning, and the blocked stairway and old passages suggested that this room had once guarded something beneath Seaton's Cove. The spirit was finally at rest, but the question that had held him here remained unanswered.

**What had the Watchman been guarding?**

The party took up the lantern and returned to the active warren. The older mystery could wait a little longer. The ratlings could not.

### The Midden Nest

The confined passages eventually opened into a cavern far larger than anything the party had encountered since entering the warren. The Midden Nest sprawled beyond the entrance in a confusion of refuse piles, crude platforms, elevated walkways, and openings leading into still more tunnels. Ratlings occupied positions throughout the chamber while giant rats moved among the debris below. Whatever hope remained that they had found only a small nest disappeared at once. They had reached the center of the ratling settlement beneath Oldstairs.

A sharp whistle cut through the cavern, and everything began moving at once. Hugo struck first, sending a brilliant flash of magical light into the clustered defenders. Ratlings and giant rats staggered, blinded and disoriented, and Xalen followed with a fan of impossible colors that dropped two ratlings and several of the great rats unconscious among the refuse. As the surviving creatures tried to organize themselves, Haldir called upon the power of the storm. His **Tempest Surge** spread through the center of the nest, whipping loose boards, cloth, dust, smoke, and nesting material into a forty-foot vortex. Walkways creaked overhead as debris spun through the cavern, obscuring sight and making movement through the center increasingly difficult.

The party quickly realized what Haldir had given them. Rather than push into a cavern filled with enemies who knew every ledge and hidden passage, they could hold the approaches and force the ratlings to come through the storm. Darrak anchored the front while the others formed around him. The ratlings had numbers and the advantage of familiar terrain, but the Tempest had divided their nest and made it difficult for them to bring those numbers against the party at once. “The ones coming from that side have to go through the vortex,” someone observed, and the party settled into a controlled defense while the storm churned through the midden.

The ratlings adjusted quickly. A weighted net sailed out of the smoke and wrapped around Galborn, tangling his arms and equipment. His first attempt to tear himself free failed, and giant rats surged toward the party while he struggled. Darrak held them at the entrance, absorbing the pressure while his companions tried to keep the front from collapsing. Cyr and Xalen used the confusion differently. With the ratlings' attention fixed on the fighters and the storm, the two slipped through the shadows and debris into striking positions. Cyr came up behind one defender while Xalen stalked another. Their attacks landed almost together, and both ratlings went down before either could react.

The counterattack intensified. Choking dust spread through part of the fighting, stinging eyes and filling the air as the party worked to cut Galborn free. Cyr resisted the worst of it, but the ratlings used the disruption to launch hooked javelins from deeper within the nest. One struck Galborn as he was being freed, and another wounded Haldir. Even then, Cyr and Xalen continued using the chaos against their attackers, slipping around the fighting and striking more ratlings from concealment while Darrak refused to surrender the entrance. The ratlings could maneuver through their warrens with remarkable speed, but the Tempest had turned their own battlefield against them.

Then a ratling in blue robes appeared among the debris and elevated paths. The party recognized the source of the choking dust, and when blue flame streaked from the creature's clawed hand, there was no longer any question that they were facing more than warriors and scouts. The spell missed, but everyone understood the danger. The blue-robed ratling immediately became the priority, and when it attempted to withdraw deeper into the warren, the party pursued it with missile fire until it fell before it could escape.

Cyr barely had time to appreciate the victory. A ratling hidden in the confusion slipped behind him and drove a blade into his back. Cyr staggered from the wound as his attacker immediately turned and fled through the debris, relying on the same speed and hidden paths that had served the ratlings throughout the battle. Cyr recovered, raised his bow, and sent an arrow after it. The shot caught the fleeing ratling before it could disappear into the tunnels. Xalen and his badger were already closing from another direction, but they never reached it. The creature collapsed beneath Cyr's arrow.

By then the fight had turned decisively. Galborn was free and cutting down the remaining giant rats at the front, the blue-robed spellcaster was dead, and the defenders emerging from the storm were increasingly isolated. The Tempest still tore through the center of the Midden Nest, but no fresh wave of ratlings appeared from within it. Slowly, the sounds of battle gave way to the rush of Haldir's storm and the settling debris around the party.

They had broken the defense of the Midden Nest, but they had paid for it. Galborn and Haldir had been struck by the ratlings' hooked weapons, Darrak had endured repeated attacks while holding the line, and Cyr carried a deep wound from the ambusher's blade. More importantly, the cavern had revealed just how extensive the warren truly was. Passages still led away into the darkness, and the party had no way of knowing how many ratlings remained below Oldstairs.

### The Missing Dockhand

With the fighting in the Midden Nest finally quiet, the party advanced cautiously into the adjoining chamber. The two-tailed feline appeared from the passage ahead and padded toward them with the same unhurried confidence she had shown since entering the cistern. Cyr looked down at her. “Have we done well?” he asked. She offered no spoken answer, but the response was unmistakable: a sense of approval and satisfaction that Cyr felt rather than heard.

The chamber beyond was lower and quieter than the Midden Nest, though the smell of wet fur and mildew remained. Makeshift cages built from broken crates, scavenged boards, and twisted wire stood against the walls. Six cats were imprisoned inside. They were frightened and hungry, but when the two-tailed feline approached, their behavior changed. The cats quieted and watched her closely; some began to purr, and none bolted when their cages were opened. They gathered around the strange feline instead, accepting her presence without hesitation. For Cyr, it was another indication that whatever she was, the cats of Seaton's Cove seemed to know her better than he did.

There was someone else in the chamber. A man sat bound and gagged nearby, his wrists and ankles tied tightly and his face bruised and crusted with dried blood. He was exhausted and dehydrated but very much alive. Even before the party freed him, he was kicking furiously toward a crude ratling idol just beyond his reach, an ugly construction of lashed skulls, tail bones, and scavenged refuse. Once they removed the gag and restraints, they learned they had found **Harl Netbinder**, Lurla's husband, missing from Saltwharf for the past three weeks.

Finding Harl and the cats should have been reason enough to turn around. The party was already carrying wounds from the Midden Nest, and the ratlings they had killed represented undeniable evidence of what had been living beneath the town. They could return to Seaton's Cove, bring Harl home to Lurla, recover their strength, and come back with guards or other reinforcements. But the passages continued beyond the prison chamber, and no one could say how many ratlings had escaped the battle or what else remained deeper in the warren. Leaving now would give any survivors time to flee, reinforce their defenses, or seal the passages behind them.

The party tended its injuries and considered its remaining resources. Merrick still had healing available, and they had potions and the Wand of Healing if the situation worsened. They also began cutting the tails from the slain ratlings. The trophies were unpleasant, but there would be little argument in Seaton's Cove about what the party had encountered once a sack of three- or four-foot ratling tails was emptied before the town authorities. After weighing the risks, they decided not to withdraw. Harl, despite his captivity and injuries, remained with them as they prepared to continue into the unexplored passages beneath Oldstairs.

### Beneath the Warren

Beyond the prison chamber, the character of the tunnels began to change. The cramped earthen burrows and makeshift ratling construction gradually gave way to gravel, broken stone, and sections of worked masonry. The pale flame of the Watchman's Lantern now accompanied the party, casting its cool light over walls that clearly predated the warren. At first the differences were easy to overlook beneath the dirt and rubble, but Darrak stopped more than once to examine the stonework. These passages had not been built by the ratlings. They were moving through the remains of something much older, buried beneath the present-day streets of Seaton's Cove.

The evidence became clearer as they continued. Walls appeared where no wall should have been, half swallowed by earth or broken through to make room for the newer passages. Sections of worked stone had collapsed and been cleared just enough to permit movement. Darrak examined the construction while Xalen studied the places where earth met masonry, and together they recognized that some of the openings were deliberate excavations. The ratlings had not merely stumbled into a few forgotten cellars. They had been digging through the buried structures beneath Oldstairs and connecting those spaces to their own warren.

The farther the party went, the less the place resembled a ratling den. Dirt underfoot became loose stone, then fitted masonry. The walls widened and the construction became more substantial, though centuries of settling and collapse had left sections unstable. Here the party slowed, picking its way through passages where fallen stone and old debris testified to structures that had once stood above them. The ratlings had found their way into an older layer of Seaton's Cove, and the party was now following them into it.

Ahead came the faint sound of running water.

### The Old Sewer

The passage opened into an ancient sewer running beneath Oldstairs. A narrow channel occupied the center of the stone corridor, carrying a shallow stream of water through debris that had accumulated over the centuries. Raised ledges ran along either side beneath a high, arched ceiling. The Watchman's Lantern cast its pale light across the damp masonry while the steady trickle of water replaced the scratching and chittering that had followed the party through much of the ratling warren.

Here there could be little doubt that they had passed into an older part of Seaton's Cove. The masonry was substantial but badly aged, with cracked stones, fallen sections, and rubble partially choking the watercourse. The ratlings had broken into this forgotten drainage system while expanding their tunnels, creating a route between their warren and whatever lay beyond. Their tracks and signs of passage continued through the old sewer.

The party followed the route carefully. After the Midden Nest and the discoveries that followed, no one was eager to assume that an apparently empty corridor was safe. The old sewer itself presented hazards enough: narrow footing beside the channel, damp stone underfoot, and centuries of decay surrounding them. Yet there was no sign that the ratlings had abandoned this route. They had been using it to reach something deeper beneath Oldstairs.

Following those signs brought the party to an alcove in the ancient masonry. There, instead of another rough opening hacked through stone, they found a heavy door. Painted across its face in pale blue was an outstretched hand.

There was no visible keyhole.

### The Blue Door

The door was unlike anything else the party had encountered in the ratling tunnels. A broad hand had been painted across its surface in pale blue, the fingers spread wide. At the center of the palm was a smaller glyph resembling a key, set into a shallow depression, but there was no ordinary keyhole. The party examined the door carefully before attempting to open it, wary that the strange symbol might conceal a trap.

A casting of **Detect Magic** confirmed that the glyph was enchanted. More importantly, its aura matched another object the party was carrying: the magical rat skull recovered from the ratling shaman. That connection gave them their first real clue, although exactly how the skull and the door worked together was less obvious. Several theories followed as they tried to determine what the hand, the key symbol, and the skull were intended to do.

Darrak eventually took the skull and approached the door while the others spread out and readied themselves. Wearing the skull as a talisman accomplished nothing. Placing his own hand over the key glyph produced the same result. Finally, they tried the most direct solution and pressed the rat skull itself against the rune in the center of the painted palm.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then pale green light flared through the glyph and flowed into the markings on the skull. A click sounded somewhere within the stonework, followed by the grinding of an ancient mechanism. Dust sifted down as the heavy door slowly released and swung inward.

After all their speculation, the answer had been surprisingly simple. The rat skull was the key. Beyond the doorway waited another dark chamber, and the stale air that escaped from it carried the smell of a place long sealed away. The party gathered itself and passed through, following the ratlings still deeper beneath Oldstairs.

### The Nest of Offerings

Beyond the Blue Door, the party entered a low chamber filled with the accumulated debris of the ratling cult. Bones had been gathered into deliberate piles rather than simply discarded: human and animal remains mixed with fish bones, rat skulls, and other fragments arranged in spirals and crude patterns across the floor. Broken pottery, bundles of hair, bits of wire, shells, and other worthless objects had been added to the arrangements as offerings. A long, tattered banner hung across an opening on the far side of the chamber.

After their experience with the Blue Door, the party was more deliberate about checking the chamber before disturbing anything. They corrected the order of their investigation and made use of **Detect Magic** while searching through the strange collections. Among the debris they discovered a small clay pot. Opening it revealed a lotion or salve containing tiny black flecks that looked like chips of obsidian or dark volcanic stone. Whatever its purpose, it was unusual enough that they kept it rather than leaving it among the ratlings' offerings.

Cyr's attention eventually settled on the hanging banner. Unlike the other scraps and decorations in the chamber, it moved slightly. When he approached it, he could feel air passing from somewhere beyond. The ratlings had concealed another opening behind it.

Cyr moved toward the banner to investigate.

### The Captive Cats

Cyr drew the hanging banner aside and immediately caught the smell from the chamber beyond. There was the familiar musk of rats and damp fur, but beneath it lay something harsher—an oily, chemical odor like dried lye or alchemist's salts. Three rough cages stood along the far wall, assembled from driftwood, scraps of iron, and knotted cord. Torn blankets, old bones, and fish spines covered portions of the floor, while little bone tokens dangled from the cages.

Inside were more of the missing cats.

Unlike frightened animals suddenly confronted by strangers, they made almost no sound. They remained inside the cages with their tails curled around them, watching Cyr and the others carefully. Before anyone touched the cages, the party searched the chamber for traps. None were found. **Detect Magic** revealed nothing magical among the cages or their contents either. Whatever purpose the ratlings had intended for these animals, there was no enchantment holding them there.

The party opened the cages and released the cats. After everything Cyr had seen since the first animals disappeared from Saltwharf, finding another group alive was welcome, but the discovery also made the ratlings' purpose more troubling. These cats had not simply wandered underground or been taken for food. Someone had deliberately captured them, built cages to hold them, and brought them through the Blue Door into the deeper shrine.

Before the party could investigate further, another sound reached them from beyond the chamber.

Someone was chanting.

### Chanting Beyond the Door

The chanting came from behind a stout door leading deeper into the shrine. After what they had already found—the ritual offerings, the cages, and the missing cats—the party had little reason to believe anything harmless waited on the other side. Cyr checked the door for traps and found none, though it was locked. While the others prepared themselves, Brother Merrick called upon his god and cast **Bless**, strengthening the party for whatever confrontation was about to follow.

Galborn did not wait for a careful plan.

The ranger stepped forward and kicked the door in. Wood and iron gave way under the blow, and before the rest of the party had fully reacted, Galborn was already rushing through the opening toward the chanting.

Beyond was a broad ritual chamber thick with heat, incense, musk, and rot. Ratlings were gathered before a grotesque idol that dominated the room—a huge construction of timbers, stone, resin, and bone shaped into a monstrous rat. Its gaping mouth bristled with needle-like fangs, and numerous blue gemstone eyes caught the chamber's light. Darkness seemed to gather unnaturally within its open jaws.

Galborn charged straight toward the ritual.

Then they went after him.

### The Chamber of the Black Fang

The others rushed through the broken doorway after Galborn and found the chamber already erupting into motion. Ratling cultists broke from their ritual positions around the idol, some reaching for weapons while others scattered toward the edges of the room. Galborn had driven straight into their midst, forcing the party to react quickly rather than establish a defensive position as they had in the Midden Nest.

Xalen cast **Faerie Fire**, outlining four of the ratlings and making them easy to follow through the dim chamber. The ratlings answered with the tactics the party had already learned to expect. Nets came sailing through the confusion, and one wrapped around Merrick, dragging at his arms and interfering with his movement. Cyr remained nearer the rear of the party as the fighters pushed into the chamber, looking for a clear target amid the sudden melee.

Then someone noticed the figure behind the idol.

It was roughly human in size, but there was something wrong with the way the chamber's light fell across it. Rather than revealing its features, the light seemed to disappear into its outline. The figure stood behind the enormous rat idol while the fighting spread around it, more shadow than person, watching from the edge of the ritual.

For the moment, no one knew what it was.

There was little time to find out. Galborn was already engaged among the cultists, Merrick was struggling against the net, and the ratlings were moving to surround the intruders. The party had entered the heart of the Black Fang shrine, and the ritual they had interrupted was rapidly becoming a battle.

### Battle in the Shrine

Cyr found his opening and fired into the ritual. One of the ratlings was clutching a scroll, marking it as an immediate threat, and Cyr's shot brought the creature down before it could make use of whatever it carried. A second ratling was wounded as the party pressed into the chamber. Around them, the fight became increasingly confused as warriors and cultists tried to recover from Galborn's sudden charge while the adventurers pushed in from the doorway.

{{< journal-image
    src="images/campaigns/assets/monsters/M-Card-Ratling Shaman.webp"

Merrick worked to free himself from the net while the others kept the ratlings occupied. Harl, battered from his captivity but unwilling to remain behind, joined the fighting with a spear. The party tried to maintain pressure on the cultists while keeping an eye on the shadowy figure behind the idol. With the strange figure still partially concealed, they used another brilliant flash near the statue, hoping the sudden light would expose it or at least reveal what they were facing.

For an instant, the chamber blazed with light.

When their vision cleared, the shadowy figure was gone.

There was no obvious passage through which it could have escaped and no clear indication of where it had gone. Whether it had slipped away in the confusion, vanished through magic, or remained somewhere nearby was impossible to tell. The party could not afford to search for it while the ratlings were still attacking.

The battle continued around the towering idol. Cyr kept firing into the ratlings while Galborn and the others fought at close quarters, gradually breaking the cultists' defense. Merrick finally cut himself free of the net and rejoined the fight. What had begun as an organized ritual was collapsing into a desperate struggle around the shrine.

Then something moved inside the idol.

A churning mass poured from its gaping mouth and spilled across the floor. At first it seemed like darkness flowing out of the statue, until the shapes separated into hundreds of small bodies.

Rats.

### The Idol Awakens

The rats struck the floor in a writhing mass and immediately spread through the chamber. They swarmed around boots and over debris, forcing the party to divide its attention between the remaining ratlings and the creatures now pouring from the idol. The rescued cats reacted at once. They rushed into the swarm, clawing and biting among the rats, while Haldir's companions joined the fight. Bendave snapped at anything that came within reach, and the badger tore into the mass wherever it gathered thickest.

The two-tailed feline entered the battle as well. Until now she had guided, watched, and occasionally offered Cyr those strange impressions that passed for communication between them. Here there was nothing distant about her purpose. She attacked the rats alongside the other cats, moving through the confusion with a speed and precision that made it clear why the ratlings had feared her from the beginning.

The party still had the cultists to contend with, but the idol was becoming impossible to ignore. What had seemed at first to be a grotesque centerpiece of the shrine began to move. Its enormous head shifted, the needle-filled jaws opened and closed, and the structure of bone, wood, stone, resin, and filth pulled itself into motion. The darkness Cyr had noticed inside its mouth was no longer merely an unsettling feature of the carving.

The Black Fang idol had awakened.

The party turned its attacks toward the thing as it lashed out at anyone who came near. Cyr continued firing while the others closed around it, but the idol was far more dangerous than the ratlings that had worshiped before it. The fight had changed in an instant. They were no longer simply destroying a cult beneath Oldstairs; they were fighting the object of its worship.

Galborn was among those closest to the idol when its jaws suddenly opened wide.

The great rat head lunged toward him.

### Swallowed Whole

The idol's jaws snapped around Galborn before he could get clear. For an instant he was caught between the needle-like teeth; then the enormous rat head jerked backward and swallowed him. The ranger disappeared into the grotesque construction of bone, wood, resin, filth, and something disturbingly organic.

Galborn was still alive. Trapped inside the idol, he drew his dagger and began hacking at it from within despite having almost no room to maneuver. There was no pool of acid or burning digestive fluid inside the thing. Instead, rats crawled through the cramped interior, biting at him while he tried to cut his way free.

Seeing Galborn disappear changed Cyr's priorities. He abandoned his position farther back in the chamber and charged the idol. With Merrick's blessing still upon him, Cyr drove his weapon into the creature and landed a powerful blow, but getting close enough to strike exposed him to the idol's counterattack. It struck Cyr hard in return, leaving him badly wounded.

The others piled onto the creature. Haldir attacked with his spear while Bendave tore at the idol from below. Harl hurled his spear at the thing but missed amid the confusion. The two-tailed feline abandoned the rat swarm and sprang onto the idol itself, climbing the rough construction until she reached its head. She raked at the cluster of blue eyes with her claws and bit at whatever she could reach.

Inside, Galborn continued fighting. His dagger finally bit deeply into the idol from within, but the rats trapped beside him were doing the same to him. The party could hear the violence inside the construct even when they could no longer see their companion.

They needed to break the idol open before it killed him.

### Darrak Falls

The party concentrated everything it had on the idol. Galborn remained trapped somewhere inside, fighting in the cramped darkness while rats tore at him, and Cyr was already badly wounded from his charge. Around them, the rescued cats, Bendave, and the badger continued struggling with the remaining rats, leaving the others free to focus on bringing the monstrous construct down.

Darrak stayed in the fight despite the punishment the party had already taken. The dwarf had held the line in the Midden Nest and fought his way through the warrens with the others, but the Black Fang idol struck with a force unlike anything they had faced below Oldstairs. One of its attacks caught him solidly and dropped him to the floor. Darrak was still alive, but only barely.

Now Merrick had two problems demanding his attention. Cyr needed healing if he was going to remain in the fight, while Darrak needed immediate aid simply to survive. Merrick used his healing on Cyr and then turned his attention toward the fallen dwarf as the others tried to keep the idol occupied. There was no opportunity to withdraw and regroup. Galborn was still inside the creature, Darrak lay bleeding on the floor, and the idol continued attacking anyone who came within reach.

From inside the construct came another burst of movement as Galborn's dagger struck from within. The ranger was still fighting, but the rats trapped inside with him continued biting and tearing at him. Every moment the idol remained standing increased the chance that the party would lose one of its companions.

Cyr, Haldir, Harl, and the others kept attacking while the two-tailed feline clung to the idol's head, tearing at its eyes. Merrick worked desperately over Darrak. The fight had ceased to be about stopping the ratling ritual or discovering what lay beneath Oldstairs.

They had to destroy the Black Fang and get Galborn out.

### The Black Fang Shattered

The party kept hammering at the idol while Merrick tended to Darrak. Cyr remained in the fight despite his wounds, and Haldir, Harl, Bendave, and the others continued attacking wherever they could find an opening. Above them, the two-tailed feline clung to the idol's head and tore at its eyes. Inside, Galborn continued carving into the construct with his dagger, attacking it from the one place it could not easily defend.

Under the combined assault, the Black Fang finally began to come apart. Pieces of wood and bone broke free, resin cracked along its body, and the structure that had seemed so formidable moments before started to collapse. The party continued striking until the animated idol could no longer sustain itself. With a final splintering of its grotesque frame, the Black Fang broke apart and crashed down amid the wreckage of the shrine.

Galborn was pulled from the remains alive, battered from the rats and his imprisonment inside the idol but still conscious. Around the chamber, the remaining swarms were driven off or destroyed, and the last resistance of the ratling cult came to an end. Merrick and the others immediately turned their attention to the wounded, particularly Darrak, whose injuries had brought him dangerously close to death.

Only after everyone had been accounted for did the party begin looking around the ruined shrine. The ritual had been stopped, the cultists defeated, and their monstrous idol destroyed, but one question remained unanswered. The shadowy figure they had seen behind the Black Fang was nowhere among the dead. The party searched the chamber and considered whether some concealed route might explain its disappearance, but they could not determine where it had gone.

As the noise of battle faded, the chamber became strangely quiet. The broken remains of the Black Fang lay scattered across the floor, its blue eyes dark. The rescued cats began emerging from the edges of the room and gathering together.

At their center stood the two-tailed feline.

### The Cat Who Walked With Shadows

The cats gathered around the two-tailed feline while the party recovered from the battle. One by one, they approached her and then slipped away into the surrounding darkness. There was no panic in them now. The animals that had been trapped in cages only a short time before seemed perfectly content to follow wherever she led, disappearing until only the strange feline remained before Cyr.

Cyr had followed her from Saltwharf to the forgotten cistern, through the ratling warren, and finally into the Black Fang shrine. She had watched him, guided the party, fought beside them, and helped protect the cats the ratlings had taken. Yet through all of it, Cyr still did not know what she was or why she had chosen him.

Something small appeared at his feet.

Cyr bent down and picked up a brass talisman fashioned in the shape of a cat's eye, its narrow pupil forming a slit through the center. As he held it, warmth spread through the metal. Then, for the first time, the feline's meaning came to him with perfect clarity.

“It is my seal.”

Cyr looked at her.

“I am Tikkit, the Cat Who Walks in Shadows.”

The name finally gave an identity to the mysterious creature that had been following him since the docks. Tikkit told Cyr that the talisman was a gift from her lord, **Rexfelis, the Cat Lord**, given in recognition of what Cyr had done for the cats of Seaton's Cove.

Cyr closed his hand around the talisman. The investigation had begun with missing dock cats, a marked rat skull, and something strange moving beneath Saltwharf. It had led him to Tikkit, the ratling warren, and a cult hidden beneath Oldstairs. The Black Fang was destroyed and the cats were free, but the party was not yet ready to return to the surface.

There were still unexplored passages beyond the shrine.

### The Room of Whispers

Rather than return immediately to the surface, the party continued exploring the chambers beyond the ruined shrine. The ratlings had clearly occupied these spaces, but the deeper they went, the less the surrounding construction resembled anything the creatures could have built. In the pale light of the Watchman's Lantern, worked stone replaced crude excavation, and older carvings appeared beneath the scratches, symbols, and fetishes the ratlings had added during their occupation.

Then the party began to hear voices.

They were faint at first, little more than murmurs at the edge of hearing. As the party entered the chamber, the sound grew into countless overlapping whispers that seemed to come from the stone itself. The voices rose and fell around them, never quite becoming clear enough for most of the party to understand. Haldir listened more carefully and recognized the language.

“Flan.”

That discovery changed their understanding of what they had found beneath Oldstairs. The ratlings had not created these chambers. They had occupied something far older. Ancient Flan markings remained on the stone, while the ratlings' crude symbols had been scratched or painted over them. Whatever purpose this place had originally served, the Black Fang cult had come later and claimed it for its own.

The party moved carefully through the whispering chamber, examining the walls without disturbing more than necessary. What had begun as Cyr's search for the missing cats of Saltwharf had now carried them beneath the ratling warren and into ruins connected to people who had occupied this land long before the present town of Seaton's Cove.

At the far side of the chamber, they found another archway.

This one had been deliberately warded.

### The Warded Arch

The archway had once been part of the same ancient complex as the whispering chamber, but the ratlings had altered it. Strings of tiny bones, claws, pieces of rat skull, and knotted fetishes hung across the opening, forming a crude curtain over the older stonework. Beneath those additions, the party could see carved Flan glyphs worked into the arch itself. The two sets of markings did not belong together. The ratling wards had been placed over something that was already ancient when the creatures found it.

The party examined the arch carefully. Beyond it, the passage continued downward at a gentle slope into worked stone, disappearing into darkness beyond the reach of the Watchman's Lantern. The whispers followed them to the threshold, mingling with the faint clatter of the ratling charms hanging across the opening.

No one crossed.

They had already fought their way through the warren, rescued Harl and the missing cats, broken the Black Fang cult, and nearly lost Darrak and Galborn in the process. More importantly, the arch represented something entirely different from the ratlings they had come below Oldstairs to find. Whatever lay beyond belonged to the older ruins, and the party knew too little about the Flan markings or the purpose of the wards to disturb them blindly.

For now, the warded arch would remain closed to them.

The party had reached the end of its exploration, but not the end of what they had discovered beneath Oldstairs.
